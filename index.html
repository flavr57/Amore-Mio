Your Business Can Legally Put Your Teenagers on the Payroll
If you run your own business, you can hire your teenagers as actual W-2 employees — paying them real wages for real work like managing social media, handling invoices, doing inventory, or running errands directly tied to the business. The tax math can be meaningful: wages you pay them are deductible to the business, and if their total earned income stays below the standard deduction threshold (which shifts year to year, so treat any figure you read online as approximate), they may owe little to no federal income tax on those earnings. For a sole proprietorship or single-member LLC wholly owned by one parent, wages paid to a child under 18 are exempt from FICA and FUTA payroll taxes under IRC Section 3121(b)(3) — that exemption is the real engine here, letting income leave your higher bracket without the usual payroll-tax bite. The details matter: the job must be real and age-appropriate, the pay must be reasonable for the work performed, and you need documentation — a written job description, timesheets, and actual paychecks or direct deposits, not cash across the kitchen table. Pull up IRS Publication 15 (Circular E) and search 'family employees' alongside your specific business entity type — and since this is general information and these rules genuinely shift and interact with state payroll requirements in ways that matter, this is exactly the kind of thing to run by your CPA before you set it up, because the wrong structure can undo the benefit entirely. Your Orchid Is Telling You Something — Here Is How to Read It
As the seasons shift, orchids often react before you notice the change — spongy, silver-green aerial roots signal healthy moisture, while yellowing lower leaves typically mean the plant is completing a natural rest cycle rather than declining. In low-humidity coastal climates, a weekly misting of the aerial roots rather than a deep drench keeps Phalaenopsis orchids thriving through warm, dry stretches. If yours has finished blooming, cut the spike back to the second node from the base, move it into bright indirect light, and give it a few quiet weeks — a second flush is more likely than most people realize.
