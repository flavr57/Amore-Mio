How DRIPs Put Every Dividend Dollar Back to Work Automatically
A dividend reinvestment plan — a DRIP — is a program offered by many publicly traded companies and brokerages that automatically uses your dividend payments to purchase additional shares, including fractional ones, instead of depositing cash into your account. Over long holding periods, these plans have been one of the quieter engines of compounding wealth: historically, reinvested dividends have accounted for a significant portion of total stock market returns, particularly in dividend-rich sectors where yields have generally run in the 2–5% range. The mechanism is elegantly simple — each quarter's payout buys a sliver more of what you already own, so your share count grows steadily without you lifting a finger. The main thing to watch is that reinvested dividends are still taxable events in most brokerage accounts even though you never see the cash, which can create a bookkeeping burden at tax time. A good next research step: compare DRIP programs across Fidelity, Vanguard, and Charles Schwab, and look at how long-tenured dividend payers like Johnson & Johnson, Coca-Cola, and Realty Income have historically structured their plans — then run a dividend-focused screen to explore yield history and payout consistency across sectors. Bougainvillea's Secret to a Spectacular Fall Bloom: Strategic Summer Stress
Bougainvillea's fiery bracts — those papery petals in magenta, coral, and orange — are triggered not by abundance but by mild water stress, which makes late summer the perfect moment to ease off irrigation and let the plant sense the seasonal shift approaching. If yours has been growing lush and generously green but refusing to bloom, the fix is counterintuitive: water a little less, keep it in full sun, and resist any urge to feed it nitrogen, which pushes foliage at the direct expense of color. In a climate like Hermosa Beach's — or Portugal's warm coastal south — this small adjustment can result in one of the most extravagant displays a wall or pergola can offer come October.
