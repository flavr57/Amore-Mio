The Safe-Harbor Rule That Keeps IRS Penalties Off Your Quarterly Bill
When you're self-employed, the IRS expects you to pay income and self-employment tax as you earn it — four times a year, via quarterly estimated payments, typically due in mid-April, mid-June, mid-September, and mid-January. The safe-harbor provision is the mechanism that protects you from underpayment penalties: if you pay in at least 100% of the prior year's total tax liability — or closer to 110% if your adjusted gross income clears a certain higher threshold (the exact figure shifts with legislation, so always verify the current number) — the IRS generally cannot assess a penalty, even if you end up owing significantly more when you file. The elegance is that you're pegging your payments to a number you already know — last year's return — rather than trying to forecast income that, for anyone running their own shop, can genuinely swing from year to year. The honest trade-off: if this year turns out dramatically more profitable, you may face a large balance due in April — you're buying penalty protection, not a smaller tax bill. To get into the actual mechanics, pull up IRS Form 2210 ('Underpayment of Estimated Tax by Individuals, Estates, and Trusts') and IRS Publication 505 ('Tax Withholding and Estimated Tax') — both are more readable than their names suggest, and they're the authoritative sources on what counts toward the threshold. These rules shift from year to year, and a CPA should sign off on which version of the calculation fits your specific situation before you act on any of this — that's not boilerplate, it's just a friend being straight with you. Late August Is the Moment to Shape Your Bougainvillea for Autumn Color
Bougainvillea blooms on new growth, which means a strategic trim now — after the main summer flush — encourages the plant to push fresh branches that will carry color well into autumn and through mild winters in coastal Southern California. Keep cuts light and targeted: remove spent bracts and any crossing or overcrowded stems, but resist hard pruning, which can delay the next bloom cycle by weeks. A light application of a low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus fertilizer at this stage supports root development and sets up the next wave of color without pushing leafy growth at the expense of blooms.

Three Bedroom Plants That Quietly Work for You While You Sleep
Snake plants (Sansevieria) and peace lilies are among the few houseplants that continue releasing oxygen after dark rather than pausing photosynthesis at night, making them genuinely useful in bedrooms rather than purely decorative. Spider plants are exceptional quiet workers too — NASA's clean air studies identified them as effective filters of formaldehyde and xylene, common off-gassing culprits in modern furniture and flooring. Placing two or three mid-sized specimens near a bedroom window gives them enough light to thrive while putting their air-filtering properties exactly where you spend a third of your life.
