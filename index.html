Not All Dividends Are Created Equal — the Tax Gap That Matters
When a company pays you a dividend, the IRS draws a sharp line between two categories: qualified dividends, taxed at the same preferential long-term capital gains rates as appreciated stock (historically somewhere in the 0–20% range depending on your bracket), and ordinary dividends, stacked on top of your regular income and taxed at whatever rate that pushes you into. The gap between those two treatments has historically run in the range of 10–20 percentage points for middle-to-upper earners — real money at scale, compounded over years. The mechanism that earns 'qualified' status is narrow but learnable: the dividend must come from a U.S. corporation or eligible foreign company, and the underlying shares must have been held for more than 60 days around the ex-dividend date. The key trade-off is that certain structures simply don't qualify regardless of how long you hold them — REITs, master limited partnerships, and many money market funds pay ordinary dividends by nature, so a portfolio built entirely around yield can inadvertently tilt toward the less-favored tax treatment. A concrete starting point: pull up any Form 1099-DIV from your brokerage and compare box 1a (total ordinary dividends) against box 1b (qualified dividends), then look at how dividend-focused ETFs like Vanguard's VIG or Schwab's SCHD report that same split in their annual distribution notices. Tax rules shift with legislation and income thresholds, so confirming where the current brackets actually land with a CPA before making any moves is the smart play. November Is the Right Moment to Reset Your Orchid for a Fresh Bloom
As daylight shortens and indoor heating pulls humidity down, most phalaenopsis orchids finish their bloom cycle and enter what looks like dormancy but is actually preparation. This is the right time to trim the spent spike just above the second node from the base, ease watering back to every ten days or so, and move the plant somewhere that gets a nightly temperature drop of around ten degrees — that thermal cue is the classic trigger for a new spike. With patience through winter, a healthy phalaenopsis will usually reward you with a fresh arch of flowers by late January.

Rest Your Bougainvillea Now for a Spectacular Southern California Spring
In coastal Hermosa Beach, bougainvillea rarely goes fully dormant, but late November is a natural cue to ease off watering and suspend fertilizer until March — a deliberate rest that concentrates energy for the spring flowering show. If nights dip unusually cold and you bring it indoors briefly, resist compensating with more water: bougainvillea thrives on a little controlled stress, and waterlogged roots in cool weather are its most common undoing. Worth knowing for the move ahead: Portugal's Algarve and Lisbon coasts are among the finest bougainvillea climates in Europe, so whatever you're practicing now will serve you beautifully there.

The Stray Who Became America's Most Decorated War Dog of All Time
Sergeant Stubby, a stray pit bull mix who wandered into a Connecticut army training camp in 1917, went on to serve in seventeen Western Front battles, detect incoming poison gas before his human unit could smell it, and personally apprehend a German spy by holding the man's trousers until soldiers arrived. He was ceremonially promoted to sergeant — formally outranking his own handler, Corporal J. Robert Conroy — and returned home to march in victory parades and meet three sitting presidents. A century on, every word of it is documented, and the story still reads like something a child invented on a very good afternoon.
