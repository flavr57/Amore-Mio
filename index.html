Your Leftover 529 Has a New Trick Under SECURE 2.0
Under the SECURE 2.0 Act, unused money in a 529 college savings plan can now roll directly into the beneficiary's Roth IRA — tax-free and penalty-free — up to a lifetime cap that runs in the neighborhood of $35,000 per beneficiary. For a business owner who has been funding 529s since the kids were small, that's potentially tens of thousands of dollars per child that sidestep the usual 10% penalty on non-education withdrawals and land instead in a Roth, where the money compounds tax-free for decades. The mechanism is clean: because 529 contributions are already after-tax dollars, the rollover counts as a Roth contribution rather than a conversion, meaning no new tax event and no income-limit test to clear. The constraint worth knowing is that the account must have been open at least 15 years before any rollover, and each year's transfer is capped at that year's Roth contribution limit, so you're chipping away gradually rather than moving the whole balance at once. These rules are still relatively new and the IRS continues to issue clarifying guidance, so — as a friend would say — have your CPA run this through your specific situation before you file or move anything. To dig in, search 'SECURE 2.0 Act Section 126' alongside IRS Publication 970; that combination surfaces both the statutory language and the most current agency interpretation. India's Currency Ban at Ten: What the Bold Experiment Taught Us
Ten years after Prime Minister Modi's overnight decision to cancel 86% of India's banknotes, economists and citizens are still tallying the results — the demonetization targeted black money and tax evasion, but many of its promised gains remain deeply contested and hard to measure. What it undeniably accelerated was India's digital payments revolution: a nation that had relied heavily on cash was pushed, by necessity, toward UPI, mobile wallets, and electronic transactions that have since transformed how hundreds of millions of people move money. The anniversary is a useful case study in the gap between sweeping policy ambition and real-world outcomes, especially in large informal economies where cash is both infrastructure and culture.

Novaya Gazeta — one of Russia's most respected investigative newspapers, whose former editor Dmitry Muratov received the Nobel Peace Prize — has shuttered its Moscow newsroom after Russian authorities labeled it a 'foreign agent' and ordered it to vacate its premises. Ten years after a community-led ban on seabed dredging was put in place around the Scottish islands, the ancient maerl beds — rare pink seaweed formations that underpin entire marine ecosystems — are thriving again, and fish populations have rebounded along with them. November Is Bougainvillea's Secret Season in Southern California
In Southern California's Mediterranean rhythm, November is secretly one of bougainvillea's finest months — the summer heat has broken, nights are cooling, and the plant's natural response to mild drought stress is to push out vivid new bracts in shades of magenta, coral, and deep orange. The key move right now is to ease back on irrigation: let the soil dry out thoroughly between waterings, mimicking the dry-season cues bougainvillea evolved to respond to with bloom. If yours is in a pot on a sunny wall or deck, make sure it's still catching at least six hours of direct light — a south- or west-facing exposure in Hermosa Beach this time of year will reward you generously.

The Difference Between Strong Seedlings and Leggy Ones Starts Now
If you're starting seeds indoors this November, the most common mistake isn't overwatering — it's underestimating how much soil warmth matters during germination; most seeds want surface temperatures of around 65–70°F, not just ambient room temperature, so a heat mat under your seed tray makes a meaningful practical difference. Once the first sprouts appear, move them to your brightest window or a supplemental grow light immediately — a week in dim light at this stage creates the etiolated, weak-stemmed seedlings that struggle for the rest of the season. Strong starts compound: the extra effort in week one pays dividends every week after.
