Where Your Cash Works Harder While You Sleep
A high-yield savings account at an online bank is simply a savings account — FDIC-insured, liquid, no market exposure — that pays a meaningfully higher interest rate than what traditional brick-and-mortar banks have historically offered. During periods of elevated interest rates, online high-yield accounts have generally run in the 3–5% APY range, while the typical big-bank savings account has often hovered closer to 0.01–0.5%; the gap is real and the compounding adds up. The mechanism is structural: online banks carry far lower overhead than branch-heavy institutions and pass a portion of those savings back to depositors as higher yields. The honest trade-off is that rates are variable — they float with the federal funds rate — so a yield that looks attractive today can compress meaningfully when monetary policy shifts. A concrete next step is pulling the current rate sheets and historical rate archives at Ally Bank, Marcus by Goldman Sachs, and SoFi, then comparing how each institution has moved its rates through past Fed cycles; the FDIC's own BankFind tool is also worth a look for deposit insurance verification. Tomorrow we step into money market funds — specifically Vanguard's VMFXX and Fidelity's SPAXX — and open the hood to see what's actually sitting inside them.
