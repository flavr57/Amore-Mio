When Your Bond Grows With Inflation: The Case for TIPS
Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities — TIPS — are U.S. government bonds whose principal value adjusts upward with the Consumer Price Index, meaning the bond keeps pace with rising prices rather than being quietly eroded by them. Historically, TIPS have offered real yields (the return above inflation) that have generally run anywhere from slightly negative to around 1–2%, depending on the rate environment — though that's a loose historical reference, not a current figure. The mechanism that makes them attractive is built into the structure itself: because the principal rises with CPI, the fixed coupon rate gets applied to a growing base over time, compounding the inflation hedge in a way conventional Treasuries simply don't offer. The honest trade-off is that TIPS can underperform standard government bonds when inflation stays tamer than markets expected, and they still carry real price volatility — they are not a substitute for cash. Two funds worth putting side by side are SCHP (the Schwab U.S. TIPS ETF) and VTIP (Vanguard Short-Term Inflation-Protected Securities ETF) — compare their expense ratios, average durations, and how each has behaved across different inflation environments. Late Summer Is the Moment Bougainvillea Rewards a Little Tough Love
Bougainvillea blooms most brilliantly when mildly stressed, and late August is exactly the right moment to lean into that — pulling back on watering now, allowing the soil to dry out almost completely between drinks, pushes the plant to redirect its energy toward those vivid papery bracts rather than foliage. Resume normal watering once you see fresh color developing, and the reward is typically one of the season's most striking displays. This is also excellent practice for a Portugal garden, where bougainvillea grows with an almost theatrical exuberance year-round.
