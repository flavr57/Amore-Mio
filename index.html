AMLP and MLPA: The Fund Wrappers That Quietly Bury the K-1
AMLP (the Alerian MLP ETF) and MLPA (the Global X MLP ETF) are exchange-traded funds structured as C-corporations — a deliberate legal choice that lets investors own master limited partnerships without ever receiving a Schedule K-1 tax form. Historically, MLP-focused funds in this category have distributed in roughly the 6–9% yield range, though that figure moves considerably with energy prices and fund policy, so treat it as directional rather than precise. The mechanism is straightforward: direct MLP ownership passes partnership income through to you, triggering K-1 forms notorious for arriving late, complicating amended returns, and disqualifying the investment from certain retirement accounts — but AMLP and MLPA sit between you and the underlying MLPs, absorbing the tax obligation at the corporate level and passing you a clean 1099 dividend instead. The honest trade-off is real: because the fund pays corporate income tax before distributing, the yield you receive is noticeably lower than what direct MLP ownership would generate — that structural cost is baked in. A productive next step is to pull AMLP's annual report from Alerian's website and MLPA's fact sheet from Global X, comparing expense ratios, distribution histories, and portfolio concentration to understand how the two differ in practice. Tomorrow we look at qualified dividends versus ordinary income — and why the rate gap between the two is one of the more quietly powerful tax levers available to a patient investor.

Coaxing a Phalaenopsis Back Into Bloom Through the Short Days of November
November's lower light levels are the most common reason Phalaenopsis orchids sulk through winter without reblooming — position them within two feet of a bright east- or west-facing window, where they catch gentle directional light without the leaf-scorching intensity of a south-facing sill. The real bloom trigger is a nightly temperature drop: a consistent few degrees cooler at night than during the day, ideally around 55–60°F after dark, signals the plant to initiate a flower spike over the coming weeks. Hold off on fertilizing until you see new root or leaf growth emerging, and water only when the potting medium has dried completely — most indoor orchid losses this time of year trace directly to overwatering in low-light conditions.

Getting Germination Right: What Indoor Seed Starts Need Most in Winter
If you're planning to start seeds indoors this season, the most common mistake is sowing too early and ending up with leggy seedlings that have outgrown their pots before outdoor conditions are ready — timing to your last frost date, working backwards, is the place to start. The fundamentals that actually determine success are consistent warmth at the root zone (most seeds want 65–70°F), good air circulation to prevent damping off, and a propagation mix light enough to drain freely rather than hold wet. Getting those conditions right will do more for your germination rate than any trick — seedlings started on time in proper conditions will catch and pass earlier ones within weeks.

The Border Collie Who Learned 1,000 Words and Changed Animal Science Forever
Chaser, a Border Collie from South Carolina, spent her life with animal psychologist John Pilley learning the names of objects with the focused intensity of a doctoral student — eventually distinguishing more than 1,000 unique toys, balls, and stuffed animals by name, far more than any other animal had been documented to understand.
