Buy the Equipment Now, Write It Off Now: Section 179 Explained
Section 179 is an IRS provision that lets a qualifying business deduct the full purchase price of equipment — computers, software, vehicles, machinery, even certain business-use improvements — in the year it's placed in service, rather than depreciating it across the standard five-to-seven-year schedule. The deduction limit has historically run well into six figures and, in recent years, well past a million dollars annually, though the exact ceiling shifts with legislation and should be treated as a rough compass rather than a number to bank on. The mechanism is blunt in a good way: the government wants businesses investing in their own tools, so it rewards the outlay with an immediate reduction in taxable income, meaning the equipment begins paying you back on the very first return you file. Bonus depreciation is the companion play — historically offering 100% first-year expensing on qualifying new and used property, though the percentage has been phasing down in recent years, and since these provisions genuinely move around, a CPA should weigh in on your specific timing before you sign anything with tax strategy in mind. The honest catch is that Section 179 can't create a loss: it won't push your taxable business income below zero, and if you eventually sell the equipment, depreciation recapture rules mean the IRS expects a reckoning on that recovered value. To go deeper, pull up IRS Publication 946, which covers the full depreciation landscape, and Form 4562, which is where the Section 179 election actually lives on your return — searching 'Section 179 deduction limit' and 'bonus depreciation phase-out schedule' will get you to the current-year numbers quickly. Iceland Says No: Voters Reject Resuming European Union Membership Talks
In a referendum result that surprised many observers, 52.8% of Icelandic voters rejected the government's proposal to restart EU accession negotiations, choosing to preserve the country's long-standing position outside the bloc. Iceland has been an EEA member for decades, granting it access to much of the single market without the deeper obligations of full membership — an arrangement that a slim majority apparently prefers to the political integration that formal accession would require. It's a quiet reminder that Europe's gravitational pull, while powerful, is not universal.

Late August Is Prime Time to Push Your Bougainvillea Toward One Last Flush
The counterintuitive secret to a spectacular late-summer bougainvillea bloom is gentle stress: let the soil dry out almost completely between waterings, which signals the plant to channel its energy into flowers rather than new foliage. A low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus fertilizer applied now supports that bloom push without encouraging leafy growth that won't harden before fall arrives. In a warm coastal climate like Hermosa Beach — or the sun-drenched regions of Portugal you're heading toward — a well-placed bougainvillea in full sun can keep throwing vivid color well into October if you hold the water and give it room to climb.
