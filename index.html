The College Savings Account That Quietly Trims Your State Tax Bill
A 529 plan is a state-sponsored, tax-advantaged savings account built specifically to pay for qualified education expenses — tuition, fees, books, room and board, and in many states K–12 costs and even registered apprenticeship programs, which matters a lot when you have a 16- and 18-year-old and timelines are no longer hypothetical. The federal side offers no deduction on contributions, but the often-overlooked edge is at the state level: depending on where you live, funding your state's own 529 can reduce your state income tax bill by a few hundred to a couple thousand dollars per year, with some states offering unlimited deductions on contributions — real money for a self-employed business owner already hunting for legitimate deductions. The mechanism is clean: money goes in after-tax, grows entirely free of federal and state income tax, and comes out tax-free as long as withdrawals cover qualified expenses, so compound growth runs without the usual annual drag. The honest catch is that if the funds don't get used for education — say one of your teenagers takes a different path entirely — you'll owe income tax plus a 10% penalty on the earnings portion, though recent rule changes now allow rolling unused balances (after a waiting period) into a Roth IRA, which softens that risk considerably. These rules shift with new legislation fairly regularly, so treat this as a starting point and have a CPA who knows your state and your business structure sign off before you move any money. To dig deeper, pull up IRS Publication 970 ('Tax Benefits for Education') and search the phrase 'qualified tuition programs Section 529' — then compare your state's own plan at savingforcollege.com, keeping in mind that many states require you to contribute before December 31 to claim the current-year deduction. California Commutes All 548 Death Sentences in Sweeping Historic Clemency
California Governor Gavin Newsom commuted the death sentences of all 548 people on the state's death row — historically the largest in the United States — converting each sentence to life without the possibility of parole. The decision marks the formal legal conclusion of a policy the state had been moving toward since Newsom imposed a moratorium on executions in 2019. It is the largest single act of clemency in American history and reignites a long-running national debate about the relationship between justice, finality, and the limits of state power over life.

Bougainvillea's November Secret: A Dry Spell Triggers a New Flush of Color
As days shorten in November, bougainvillea can stall and sulk — but a deliberate dry period is actually the trigger for a new round of brilliant bracts. Hold back on water for two to three weeks, let the soil go quite dry, then resume normal irrigation and you will often see fresh color within a month. In Hermosa Beach's mild autumn, with temperatures staying comfortably in the 60s and 70s, this stress-and-reward cycle is easy to manage outdoors without any risk of cold damage.

Peace Lilies and Snake Plants: Your Best Indoor Air Allies This Season
As windows stay closed more through the cooler months, indoor air quality quietly declines — and peace lilies (Spathiphyllum) and snake plants (Sansevieria trifasciata) are among the most reliable houseplants for filtering common household VOCs while tolerating low light without complaint. The peace lily will droop visibly when it needs water, making it a forgiving companion for a busy household, while the snake plant is nearly indestructible and releases oxygen through the night, which many people find improves sleep quality. Grouping several plants together in a room also nudges ambient humidity upward slightly — a small but real comfort as the air dries out in winter.
