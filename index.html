The Health Insurance Write-Off Self-Employed People Often Miss
If you're running your own business, the IRS allows you to deduct 100% of health insurance premiums you pay for yourself, your spouse, and your dependents — directly from gross income, above the line, with no need to itemize. For a family with two teenagers heading toward college visits and athletic physicals, those premiums add up fast, and so does the deduction; historically, self-employed families can shelter several thousand dollars of income annually this way, though the exact figure depends entirely on what you're actually paying for coverage. The mechanism is particularly valuable because it reduces your adjusted gross income, which cascades into other calculations — phase-outs, credits, and even the self-employment tax side of the ledger. The one honest watch-out: you cannot claim this deduction for any month you were eligible to enroll in a subsidized employer plan, including through a spouse's job, so it's worth mapping that out carefully before assuming the full year qualifies. To pull the thread yourself, look up IRC Section 162(l) and the line instructions for Schedule 1 of Form 1040; IRS Publication 535 on Business Expenses covers it in plain language as well. These rules do shift with legislation, so treat this as a conversation-starter with your CPA rather than a filing checklist — a good one will know exactly how it sits in your specific picture.
