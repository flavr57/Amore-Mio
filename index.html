GOLDEN VISA
Portugal's Golden Visa program remains open through qualifying investment funds and capital transfers following the 2023 reforms that closed the direct residential property route. Processing times have improved, and the pathway to permanent residency after five years continues to make it one of Europe's most attractive investor visa programs.
~
COST OF LIVING
Lisbon and Porto have settled into a new, higher price band — groceries, dining, and services run roughly 25–35% higher than three years ago. The good news: the Alentejo, Setúbal coast, and Silver Coast remain genuinely affordable, offering quality of life that rivals Lisbon at a fraction of the cost.
✓
NHR TAX REGIME
Portugal's IFICI regime — the successor to NHR — offers a flat 20% tax rate on Portuguese-sourced income for qualifying new residents, including researchers, highly qualified professionals, and certain business founders. Those who structure foreign income carefully and enter the program early in their residency window can still capture meaningful tax advantages.
~
HOUSING MARKET
Central Lisbon rentals remain tight, with one-bedrooms in Príncipe Real or Chiado running €1,800–€2,500 per month and competition brisk. Secondary cities like Braga, Évora, and Setúbal offer well-priced apartments with fast rail links to Lisbon — and increasingly, an expat community to go with them.

Europe's Great Rivers Run Dry in a Summer of Historic Drought
The Rhine, Danube, and Po have all fallen to record or near-record low levels this summer, triggering a cascade of economic and environmental consequences across the continent. Cargo transport on the Rhine — a critical artery for Central European commerce — has been severely curtailed, and hydroelectric output has dropped precisely when energy demand is at its peak. The crisis is a sharp reminder of how climate-driven water stress is quietly rewiring Europe's infrastructure and energy logic, with particular relevance for southern European countries, including Portugal, that rely on river and reservoir systems.

Georgia's Ancient Okefenokee Swamp Joins UNESCO's World Heritage List
The Okefenokee Swamp — one of North America's oldest, most biodiverse, and most otherworldly wetlands — has officially joined UNESCO's World Heritage list, taking its place alongside the Grand Canyon and Yellowstone as one of humanity's formally protected natural treasures. Spanning nearly 700 square miles across Georgia and Florida, the Okefenokee shelters alligators, sandhill cranes, carnivorous plants, and one of the world's largest intact freshwater peat ecosystems. The designation brings both global recognition and a stronger layer of protection to a landscape that has faced persistent threats from nearby mining interests.

This New Nickel Mine Will Absorb 1.5 Million Tons of Carbon Annually
Canada Nickel Company has received final provincial approval for an operation that inverts the usual environmental logic of mining: rather than emitting carbon, it will absorb it. The mine's geology enables mineral carbonation — a natural process in which nickel-bearing rock reacts with atmospheric CO₂ and locks it away permanently — sequestering an estimated 1.5 million tons of carbon each year during active operation. It is the kind of elegant, unexpected climate solution that reminds us how much potential still lies at the intersection of earth science and engineering ingenuity.

August Is Bougainvillea's Turning Point — Here's What It Needs Now
Your bougainvillea is likely at peak display this month, but what you do in August will shape how brilliantly it returns next season. Ease back on watering — bougainvillea blooms most extravagantly under mild stress, and overwatering pushes energy into lush leaves at the expense of those incandescent papery bracts. A light feed with a low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus fertilizer now will encourage the plant to begin setting bud sites for the autumn flush, the second and often most spectacular flowering of the year.
