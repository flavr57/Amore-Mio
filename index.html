Mozambique Shields a Butterfly Forest Teeming With 200 Rare Species
In a landmark conservation victory, the Mozambican government has officially protected 23,000 acres around Mount Mabu — one of the largest stretches of lowland rainforest in southern Africa and home to an extraordinary concentration of butterflies, birds, and plants found nowhere else on Earth. A government decree established the area as the Mount Mabu Community Conservation Area, placing local communities at the center of its stewardship going forward. For a forest scientists once called a 'lost world,' this is the kind of headline that restores a little faith in the relationship between governments and the natural world.

The Self-Employed Retirement Account That Lets You Contribute Twice
A solo 401(k) — also called a one-participant 401(k) or i401(k) — is a retirement plan built specifically for self-employed business owners who have no full-time employees other than a spouse. The double-contribution structure is what makes it worth knowing: as the employee, your elective deferrals have historically run in the low-to-mid $20,000s annually (the precise limit is indexed and shifts each year), and as the employer you can layer on a profit-sharing contribution of up to roughly 25% of your net self-employment income — pushing total annual contributions into ranges historically around $60,000–$66,000 or more, making it one of the most aggressive income shelters available to someone running their own shop. The mechanism is clean: money goes in pre-tax (or Roth, in plans that allow it), compounds without annual taxation, and retirement account balances are generally excluded from FAFSA calculations — a quiet secondary benefit that tends to surprise people with kids heading toward college. The main practical watchout is timing: you must establish the plan by December 31 of the tax year you want contributions to count, though you can fund it through your filing deadline including extensions, and once total plan assets cross a historical threshold around $250,000, IRS Form 5500-EZ becomes a required annual filing. A good CPA should sign off on your numbers before you file — the rules around eligible compensation shift more often than you'd think, and this is general information worth researching rather than a prescription to act on. Start by searching 'one-participant 401(k)' on IRS.gov and pulling up IRS Publication 560, 'Retirement Plans for Small Business,' which walks through the contribution formula and Form 5500-EZ requirements with unusual clarity. China and The Met Unlock Hidden Secrets of a Centuries-Old New York Mural
A Chinese arts and heritage conservation project undertaken to reproduce a massive historical mural has borne unexpected fruit thousands of miles away — at New York's Metropolitan Museum of Art. Albertans are voting today on whether to hold a formal referendum on separating from Canada — a question that, while polling suggests most residents would ultimately reject secession, has already proven divisive enough to reshape the province's political landscape. Brazil Votes 75-to-1 to Abolish Its Long Six-Day Workweek Forever
In a vote so lopsided it raises the obvious question of what took so long, Brazil's senate approved a constitutional amendment eliminating the country's traditional six-day workweek — bringing one of the world's largest economies in line with the five-day standard most of the developed world adopted generations ago. The near-unanimous margin reflects a broader cultural shift in how Brazilians, and much of the world, are rethinking the relationship between work, rest, and human dignity. For the millions of workers who have been clocking in on Saturdays as a simple matter of course, this is not a small thing.

Somerset Volunteers Became Salmon and Earthworms to Save Their River
In an imaginative conservation experiment in Somerset, England, community volunteers were invited to quite literally inhabit the perspective of the wildlife sharing their landscape — roleplaying as salmon, deer, and earthworms to understand the local river's needs from the inside out. The exercise anchors a 30-year restoration plan built on the premise that people protect what they can genuinely imagine, not just what they are told to value. It is the kind of story that makes you think the most practical thing we can do for the natural world might sometimes be to get beautifully weird about it.

October Is the Ideal Moment to Shape Your Bougainvillea for Winter Color
In a warm coastal climate like Hermosa Beach, October brings the sweet spot bougainvillea love: cooling nights, gentler sun, and just enough stress to trigger the next bloom cycle. This is the moment to cut back any leggy or spent growth by up to a third — it encourages a tighter, more floriferous structure — and to check that container-grown plants haven't become so root-bound that they're holding back the show. Finish with a light dose of low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus fertilizer now, and the vivid magenta or coral flush that makes these plants worth every bit of their stubbornness should arrive right on schedule.

The Best Bedroom Plants Are Working Quietly on Your Sleep All Night
Lavender remains the most research-backed choice for improving sleep through scent — its linalool content measurably reduces anxiety and lowers resting heart rate, making a small pot on a south-facing windowsill one of the most pleasant health interventions available. Jasmine (Jasminum polyanthum) earns its place for much the same reason: its evening fragrance is subtle rather than overpowering, and it thrives in bright indirect light with a thorough weekly watering. If light is limited, pothos and snake plants (Sansevieria trifasciata) are the classic workhorses — reliably cheerful, genuinely forgiving, and perfectly content with the kind of casual attention most of us can actually maintain.

When Children Read Aloud to Dogs, Something Remarkable Happens to Both
The READ program — Reading Education Assistance Dogs, founded by Intermountain Therapy Animals in 1999 — began with a simple insight: children who struggle to read aloud become measurably less anxious when their audience is a dog, because a dog does not correct, does not sigh, and does not look disappointed. Now active in thousands of libraries and schools across the United States, the program has consistently shown improvements in reading fluency, confidence, and enjoyment among participating children. And the dogs, for their part, seem to approach each session with the particular attentiveness they reserve for things that genuinely matter.
