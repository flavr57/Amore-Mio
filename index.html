The Covered Call Fund That Plays by Better Tax Rules
SPYI — formally the NEOS S&P 500 High Income ETF — generates income by systematically writing call options on the S&P 500 index itself, distributing the collected premiums as monthly cash to shareholders. Historically, funds in this newer generation of index-options ETFs have run in a range that makes them attractive income producers compared with straight dividend strategies — loosely in the high single digits to low double digits, though that figure moves with volatility and is never a promise. The key mechanism that sets SPYI apart from older covered-call funds is its use of index options rather than equity options: those gains qualify under Section 1256 of the IRS tax code, which applies a 60/40 treatment — 60% long-term, 40% short-term — regardless of how long you held the position, a meaningful advantage for taxable accounts. The honest trade-off is cap risk: in a strong bull market, if the index runs past your strike price, you have sold away some of that upside. A concrete next research thread is to pull the distribution histories and fact sheets for SPYI alongside its closest peers — XYLD from Global X, JEPI from JPMorgan, and QYLD — and compare how each fund balanced income generation against total return over the same period, then look at after-tax distributions specifically. Why Cooler October Nights Are Your Orchid's Secret Rebloom Trigger
Phalaenopsis orchids are quietly opportunistic: a consistent 10–15°F swing between daytime and nighttime temperatures is one of the most reliable signals that prompts a new bloom spike, and October in Southern California delivers exactly that without any effort on your part. Keep the plant near a bright, indirect-light window, ease back on watering as growth naturally slows, and resist the urge to fertilize until you see fresh root or leaf activity beginning to emerge. With those cooler nights doing their work, a rebloom before the new year is genuinely within reach.

Bougainvillea Blooms Most Brilliantly When You Resist the Urge to Coddle It
Bougainvillea is among the few plants that rewards deliberate restraint: it produces its most spectacular color when slightly root-bound and water-stressed, a response that tells the plant to flower before conditions deteriorate further. As autumn arrives, hold back on watering and stop fertilizing entirely — let the soil dry out more than feels comfortable, and the plant redirects that energy into vivid bracts rather than leafy green growth. A south-facing wall that retains daytime warmth is ideal for carrying those blooms well into the cooler months ahead.
