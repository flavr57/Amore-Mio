The Global Dividend Yield You're Missing — and the Tax That Bites It
VYMI — Vanguard's International High Dividend Yield ETF — holds hundreds of dividend-paying stocks headquartered outside the United States, spanning Europe, Asia-Pacific, and select emerging markets. Historically it has paid in roughly the 4–6% range, though that figure moves with currency shifts and market conditions, so treat it as a loose reference point rather than a projection. The appeal is that companies in many international markets — particularly in Europe and Asia — tend to distribute a larger share of earnings as dividends than their US counterparts, giving income-focused investors an entirely different cash-flow stream to tap. The wrinkle is withholding tax: when a German, Japanese, or Australian company pays a dividend, its government typically takes 15–30% off the top before the money reaches the fund, and if you hold VYMI inside an IRA or 401(k), you generally cannot claim the foreign tax credit on Form 1116 to recover that lost slice — making the tax-deferred wrapper that usually helps you actually work against you here, and those specifics are worth a conversation with a CPA since treaty rates and fund structures shift. As a concrete next step, pull VYMI's annual report or country-exposure fact sheet and compare its withholding drag against IDV (iShares International Select Dividend ETF) or SCHY (Schwab International Dividend ETF) to see how fund construction changes the picture. Tomorrow we're looking at Canadian bank dividends — stocks with payout histories that stretch back through recessions, crises, and decades of patient compounding.

Coax Your Bougainvillea Into a Second Bloom This September
As early fall arrives, bougainvillea can be nudged into a spectacular second flush with a counterintuitive move: ease off the water for two to three weeks until leaves look mildly stressed, then resume normal irrigation. The plant blooms most prolifically when it senses mild drought — a survival mechanism you can work with rather than fight. Follow the dry spell with a low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus fertilizer to push energy into bract development rather than leafy green growth.
