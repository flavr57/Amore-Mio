The Data Centers Holding Up the Internet — and Paying Dividends
Digital Realty Trust (DLR) and Equinix (EQIX) are real estate investment trusts that own and operate the physical buildings where the internet actually lives — server halls, fiber interconnection hubs, and hyperscale campuses leased to cloud providers, telecoms, and enterprises worldwide. As REITs, both companies are required to distribute the bulk of their taxable income to shareholders; historically, data center REITs have offered dividend yields in roughly the 1–3% range, modest by traditional REIT standards but typically paired with stronger long-term appreciation as digital infrastructure demand compounds. The income mechanism is straightforward: tenants sign long-term leases — often with built-in rent escalators — to house their servers and fiber connections, generating highly predictable, recurring cash flows. The honest trade-off is that both companies carry heavy capital expenditure burdens, since data centers are enormously expensive to build, power, and cool — and like all REITs, they feel real pressure when interest rates are elevated. A concrete research thread: pull the FTSE NAREIT data center subsector fact sheets and compare DLR's and EQIX's annual reports side by side, paying attention to tenant mix, geographic footprint, and leverage ratios. Autumn's Slight Chill Is the Secret Signal Your Orchid Has Been Waiting For
As coastal California evenings ease even a few degrees, phalaenopsis orchids often receive the temperature cue they need to initiate a new flower spike — if you give them the right conditions. Try moving yours to a spot where nighttime temperatures dip about 10°F below daytime highs, reduce watering slightly, and keep it in bright but filtered light; this mimics the seasonal shift that triggers blooming in their native subtropical habitat. Be patient: a new spike typically emerges within six to eight weeks of the cue, and then the long, graceful bloom rewards you for months.

September Is Bougainvillea's Golden Hour — Ease Off the Water and Watch It Bloom
In Hermosa Beach's warm coastal climate, September and early October are prime time for bougainvillea: the nights are still mild enough for the plant to thrive, while a slight reduction in summer watering actually encourages the brilliant bract color the plant is famous for. Bougainvillea blooms best under mild stress — ease off irrigation, hold the high-nitrogen fertilizer, and switch to a low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus blend to push flowers over foliage. A little planned neglect now sets up a spectacular show well into November.
