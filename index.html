The Pipelines That Pay You: Inside America's Midstream MLPs
Enterprise Products Partners (EPD) and Energy Transfer (ET) are two of the largest midstream Master Limited Partnerships in the United States — publicly traded partnerships owning thousands of miles of pipelines, storage hubs, and processing facilities dedicated to moving oil, natural gas, and natural gas liquids. Historically, major midstream MLPs like these have run distribution yields in roughly the 5–9% range, though yields shift with unit prices and business cycles, so any historical figure is a starting point for your own research rather than a forecast. The structure is what drives the income: MLPs are pass-through partnerships that owe no federal corporate income tax, so the cash generated by long-term, fee-based contracts — think pipeline tolls — flows predominantly to unitholders as quarterly distributions. The catch is the annual Schedule K-1 — a tax document that replaces the standard 1099, reporting your proportionate share of the partnership's income, deductions, and credits, which can complicate your return and may create filing obligations in multiple states where the partnership operates. A concrete next step is to pull the investor relations pages for both EPD and ET, compare their per-unit distribution histories over the past decade, and download a sample K-1 from their annual filings to see exactly what lands in a unitholder's tax package. Tomorrow, we look at AMLP and MLPA — the ETF wrappers built specifically to deliver MLP income without ever handing you a K-1.

Coax Your Bougainvillea Back into Bloom With a Little Benign Neglect
Bougainvillea blooms when it is stressed — specifically, when kept on the drier side and exposed to shortening days, which November delivers naturally even in Southern California. Now is the time to cut back watering significantly, hold off on any nitrogen-heavy fertilizer, and let the plant feel a touch uncomfortable; that mild drought stress is precisely what triggers the next spectacular flush of bracts. A light tidy-up after the last flush has faded will also encourage new flowering wood for the season ahead.
