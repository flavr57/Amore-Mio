Main Street Capital: When Your Portfolio Lends to Main Street
Main Street Capital (ticker: MAIN) is a business development company, or BDC — a publicly traded firm that acts like a private lender and equity investor to small and mid-sized American companies, wrapped in a structure you can buy and sell on a stock exchange like any other share. As a BDC, MAIN is required by law to distribute at least 90% of its taxable income to shareholders, which has historically supported dividends that have generally run somewhere in the 6–9% range — though that figure moves with interest rates and credit conditions, so treat it as rough historical context rather than a forecast. The mechanism is the spread: MAIN borrows capital at one rate and deploys it into loans and equity stakes at higher rates, then passes most of the difference back to investors as regular income. The honest trade-off is credit risk — if the small businesses in MAIN's portfolio hit trouble, default rates climb and distributions can be cut, as happened for many BDCs during the 2008 and 2020 downturns. To go deeper, pull MAIN's most recent 10-K and investor fact sheet on SEC EDGAR, then run a side-by-side comparison against FS KKR Capital Corp or Gladstone Investment to see how portfolio mix, leverage ratios, and fee structures differ across the category. Tomorrow we look at the two heavyweights of the BDC world — Ares Capital and Hercules Capital — where scale and specialization tell quite a different story.

Bougainvillea's Paradox: A Little Thirst Unlocks the Color Show
November in Hermosa Beach is the moment to ease back on watering your bougainvillea — counterintuitively, mild drought stress is precisely what triggers the explosive color display that makes these plants so spectacular. Pull back irrigation to once every ten to fourteen days through the cooler months, avoid nitrogen-heavy fertilizers that push leafy green growth at the expense of bracts, and let the plant feel just a touch of hardship. The reward, come late winter, is a cascade of magenta, coral, or white that looks like the plant has been set gently and gloriously on fire.

Starting November Seedlings Right: Less Water, More Light, More Patience
If you are nursing seedlings on a windowsill this month, the most common mistake is overwatering in low-light conditions — cool, damp soil invites damping off before a seedling has ever had a real chance. The advice from seasoned growers is to sow into free-draining, fine compost, water from below rather than above, and position plants where they catch the most available natural light to build compact, strong stems. Restraint at this stage pays off tenfold when it finally comes time to transplant into the open ground.
