When Your Bond Interest Skips the State Tax Line Too
A state-specific municipal bond fund holds debt issued by governments within a single state — California, New York, wherever you happen to live — and passes the interest to you exempt from both federal income tax and, crucially, that state's income tax as well. In a high-tax state like California, where the top marginal rate has historically sat in the low double digits, a fund yielding somewhere in the 3–5% range historically can translate to a meaningfully higher taxable-equivalent yield for residents in the upper brackets. The mechanism is clean: Congress and most state legislatures exclude home-state muni interest from their respective tax bases, so the income simply never appears on either return. The honest trade-off is concentration — you are betting one state's fiscal health stays intact, which matters more in places like Illinois or New Jersey than in, say, Texas. A good research thread to pull: compare Vanguard's California Long-Term Tax-Exempt Fund (ticker VCITX) with the Fidelity California Municipal Income Fund side by side, reading each fund's fact sheet for duration, credit quality breakdown, and expense ratio.
