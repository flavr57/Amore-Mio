Buying a Dollar of Assets for Eighty Cents
A closed-end fund (CEF) is a pooled investment vehicle that raises a fixed amount of capital through an IPO, lists its shares on a stock exchange, and then trades exactly like a stock — the share count never changes after launch. Income-focused CEFs have historically distributed in a rough range of around 5% to 10% or more annually, though yields vary enormously by strategy, leverage, and market conditions, so treat any figure you encounter as a starting point for research, not a promise. The essential mechanism is this: because the share count is fixed, the market price floats independently of the fund's underlying net asset value (NAV) — what the actual portfolio of securities would fetch if liquidated — meaning shares sometimes trade at a discount of 10%, 15%, or even wider, as though you are buying a basket of bonds or dividend stocks for less than their open-market value. The honest catch is that many discounts are persistent for real reasons: high management fees, the carrying cost of leverage, or structural features that resist arbitrage, so a wide discount does not automatically close in your favor. A productive next step is CEFConnect.com, which maintains historical discount and premium data across hundreds of funds — running a screen sorted by discount depth and consistency is a reasonable first cut for identifying names worth reading deeper on. Tomorrow we turn to two of the most-discussed names in this space — PIMCO's PDI and PTY — and what their substantial use of leverage actually means for income-seekers in a rising-rate environment.

Bougainvillea in November: Why This Is the Month to Let It Rest
If your bougainvillea has had a long, colorful summer on the patio, November is the moment to ease off — reduce watering significantly and hold the fertilizer entirely, allowing the plant to enter its natural semi-dormant phase. This winter rest is not neglect; it is precisely what sets the stage for the explosive flowering you want come spring, as the mild stress of cool, drier conditions triggers the hormonal shift that produces those vivid bracts. Keep it in the brightest spot available, protect it from any hard frost, and resist watering until the top few inches of soil are genuinely dry to the touch.
