GOLDEN VISA
Portugal's Golden Visa program continues to attract non-EU investors through real estate investment funds, venture capital, and job-creation routes — direct residential property purchases in Lisbon and Porto remain excluded, but qualifying fund investments are active and well-established. Processing times have improved through 2025–2026, with AIMA (the immigration agency) clearing a significant backlog.
~
COST OF LIVING
Lisbon and the Algarve continue to see elevated costs compared to just a few years ago, with groceries, dining, and services notably pricier than in Portugal's interior — though still comfortably below London or California. Porto remains the sweet spot for value-conscious expats, offering urban vibrancy at a gentler price point.
✓
NHR TAX REGIME
Portugal's updated IFICI regime (the successor to NHR, effective from 2024) offers qualifying new residents a flat 20% tax rate on Portuguese-source income and potential exemptions on certain foreign income for a 10-year period. It's particularly attractive for remote workers, pensioners, and professionals in high-value activities — worth discussing with a Portuguese tax advisor before your move.
~
HOUSING MARKET
Portugal's rental market remains tight in Lisbon and the Algarve, with demand from expats and digital nomads keeping prices elevated — expect €1,500–€2,500/month for a well-located Lisbon apartment. The government continues to roll out housing incentive programs, but supply constraints mean competition for quality rentals is still brisk heading into 2026.
