The One Soviet Officer Who Said No and Saved the World

His quiet, steady refusal may have been the single act that prevented a chain of events from which there was no return. The Fat Yield of QYLD and XYLD — and What It Costs
QYLD (Global X NASDAQ 100 Covered Call ETF) and XYLD (Global X S&P 500 Covered Call ETF) are exchange-traded funds that generate income by systematically writing covered call options on the Nasdaq 100 and S&P 500, respectively, and distributing most of that option premium to shareholders as monthly cash. Both have historically run in roughly the 9–13% annual yield range, though the actual payout shifts with market volatility and the prevailing price of options rather than following any fixed schedule. The mechanism is clean: the fund holds the underlying index stocks, sells call options near the current market price, collects the premium upfront, and pays it out — a predictable income stream that doesn't require the market to move higher. The honest trade-off is that when the Nasdaq or S&P surges, these funds can't participate in those gains beyond the option strike price, which is why both funds have historically trailed their parent indexes considerably during strong bull markets. Before forming a view, pull the Global X fact sheets for both tickers and compare their total-return charts against QQQ and SPY over a full market cycle — that gap tells you exactly what you're exchanging for the income, in dollar terms. Tomorrow we'll look at SPYI and some of the newer covered call structures that use index options instead of equity options — a design difference that quietly changes the tax story in a way worth knowing.

October Is the Month Your Bougainvillea Actually Wants Less From You
As Southern California days shorten and nights cool, bougainvillea enters its natural rest phase — and the best thing you can do is ease back on water entirely and stop fertilizing until spring. Stress, counterintuitively, is what triggers the plant's spectacular bracts: a slightly dry, slightly cool root zone sends a signal that encourages blooming rather than vegetative growth. Pull back to watering once every ten to fourteen days, let the leaves feel the season, and the plant will reward you come March with a flush of color that makes the restraint feel exactly right.

The Bedroom Plants That Quietly Earn Their Keep Every Night
A few plants pull genuine double duty as autumn settles in: snake plants (Sansevieria) perform their gas exchange at night rather than during the day, making them unusually well-suited for a bedroom, while peace lilies have been shown to filter common indoor VOCs like benzene and formaldehyde from the air around them. A small pot of lavender on a windowsill adds a third dimension — its scent has been studied for its effect on sleep quality, with several trials finding reductions in heart rate and nighttime waking among participants. As Hermosa Beach nights drift toward the cooler end of October, a bedroom with one of each becomes a quietly well-engineered place to rest.

Trained Service Dogs Are Giving Veterans With PTSD Their Lives Back
A growing body of research now confirms what veterans and their families have felt for years: pairing a trained psychiatric service dog with a veteran living with PTSD produces measurable reductions in anxiety, nightmares, hypervigilance, and social isolation.
