VYM: How Vanguard's Yield Tilt Turns the Market Into a Paycheck
VYM is Vanguard's High Dividend Yield ETF — a large, low-cost fund that holds several hundred US stocks selected because they are forecast to pay above-average dividends, then weighted by market capitalization within that filtered universe. Historically, the fund has run in roughly the 3–4% yield range, though that figure shifts with markets and should be treated as a loose historical reference rather than a forecast. The mechanism is straightforward: by screening out the lowest-yielding companies and excluding REITs (handled in separate Vanguard funds), VYM concentrates ownership in mature businesses that tend to return cash to shareholders rather than reinvesting everything into growth. The honest trade-off is sector concentration — financials, healthcare, and consumer staples tend to dominate the portfolio, which means VYM can lag meaningfully in years when high-growth technology leads the charge. A useful research thread is pulling VYM's fact sheet directly from Vanguard and running a side-by-side with SCHD, Schwab's dividend equity ETF, which layers a quality screen on top of the yield filter — a philosophically different approach worth understanding before choosing which flavor fits. Tomorrow we look at VIG and DGRO — the dividend growth funds that favor companies steadily raising their payouts over companies simply paying large ones right now.

To Make Your Bougainvillea Bloom, You Have to Learn to Stress It
Bougainvillea is one of the few plants that rewards deliberate tough love — stress watering, meaning reducing irrigation by roughly half, signals the plant to shift from leafy growth into flowering mode and coaxes out those vivid papery bracts. In coastal Southern California's September warmth, now is a well-timed moment to pull back on watering and let the plant do its work. Full sun is non-negotiable; a shaded bougainvillea will answer restraint with disappointment rather than color.

The Peace Lily Is the Quiet Overachiever of Your Indoor Garden
Peace lilies (Spathiphyllum) tolerate low light, flag their thirst dramatically before any real damage is done, and have been studied — most famously in NASA research — for their ability to filter common indoor pollutants including ammonia, benzene, and formaldehyde. They are one of the few flowering plants that thrive without direct sun, making them ideal for bedrooms where air quality matters most for sleep. If yours has not bloomed in a while, moving it just a little closer to a window — even indirect morning light — often does the trick.

The Portuguese Water Dog Carried Messages Between Boats and Won Every Heart
The Cão de Água Português spent centuries working alongside fishermen along Portugal's Atlantic coast — herding fish into nets, retrieving lost gear, and even carrying messages between vessels — a working dog in the truest, saltiest sense.
