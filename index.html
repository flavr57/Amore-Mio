GOLDEN VISA
Portugal's revamped Golden Visa program — now focused on qualifying investment funds rather than direct property purchases — continues to attract significant interest from non-EU applicants seeking European residency. Processing times have stabilized, making mid-2026 a reasonable window to begin applications.

COST OF LIVING
Lisbon and Porto remain noticeably pricier than they were five years ago, with central one-bedroom rents often running €1,500–€2,200 per month. The interior Alentejo, smaller coastal towns, and the Silver Coast still offer considerably more value for those willing to explore beyond the obvious hubs.

NHR TAX REGIME
Portugal's IFICI regime — the successor to NHR — offers qualifying new residents a 20% flat income tax rate for up to ten years, making Portugal one of Europe's more tax-competitive destinations for foreign professionals. American expats should engage a cross-border specialist early to navigate the US-Portugal tax treaty properly.

HOUSING MARKET
Portugal's property market remains competitive, particularly in Lisbon and the Algarve where inventory is tight and prices have climbed steadily. Patient buyers willing to look at the Alentejo or smaller northern towns will find the value proposition that drew so many expats here in the first place.

Europe Ends the Era of Ultra-Cheap Chinese Imports Amid Trade Talks
The EU has scrapped customs exemptions for low-value imports from China — the loophole that allowed platforms like Shein and Temu to deliver goods to European doorsteps virtually duty-free. The move coincides with talks in which Brussels secured agreements with Beijing to monitor trade flows and improve European access to rare earth materials. The shift signals a maturing European trade strategy: competing with China more assertively while keeping diplomatic channels carefully, deliberately open.
