GOLDEN VISA
Portugal's Golden Visa remains active via investment fund contributions (€500K), cultural projects (€250K), and job creation — real estate in major cities was removed as a qualifying route in 2023, but the program is processing applications and the fund route continues to be the most popular pathway for non-EU applicants. Processing times have improved noticeably in 2025–2026.
~
COST OF LIVING
Lisbon and Porto remain meaningfully more affordable than comparable US coastal cities, though rents have risen sharply over the past three years — a Lisbon one-bedroom now averages €1,600–2,000 per month. Groceries, dining out, and utilities stay well below US equivalents, keeping the overall value proposition compelling for Americans who relocate.
~
NHR TAX REGIME
Portugal's original Non-Habitual Resident program closed to new applicants in early 2024 and was replaced by IFICI — a more targeted regime offering a flat 20% income tax rate for qualifying professionals, researchers, and entrepreneurs for 10 years. Those who registered under NHR before the cutoff retain their benefits; new arrivals should consult a Portuguese tax advisor about IFICI eligibility early in the process.
✗
HOUSING MARKET
Portugal's rental market — especially in Lisbon, Porto, and the Algarve — remains one of the tightest in Western Europe, with international demand continuing to outpace supply. Property purchase prices have slightly moderated from 2023 peaks, but securing a quality long-term rental requires patience; starting your search three to four months before arrival is strongly advisable.

California Condor Soars Into Oregon for the First Time in 122 Years
Last month, a California condor — a bird that came within 27 individuals of extinction in the 1980s — flew north into Oregon for the first time since 1904, a solo voyage of several hundred miles before returning home to Redwoods National Park. It is one of conservation's most stirring comeback stories: from nine birds in captivity to a wild population now numbering over 500. That a condor can now casually stretch its wings into new territory is the most hopeful kind of proof that the work was worth it.

Mangrove Forests Are Growing Back — More and Denser Than Before
A landmark global study has found that worldwide mangrove forest loss has not only halted but reversed, with more total mangrove area today than existed 20 years ago — and the forests are denser and more carbon-rich than before. Mangroves are among the planet's most efficient carbon stores and serve as nurseries for marine life, buffers against coastal flooding, and living filters for coastal water. This is the rare environmental headline where the news is simply: we fixed it.

Nasturtiums: The Easiest, Most Rewarding Edible Flower You Can Grow
Cheerful and almost comically generous, nasturtiums thrive in poor soil with minimal water — qualities that make them ideal for both California's dry summers and Portugal's sun-baked patios alike. Sow seeds directly into the ground in a sunny spot and within weeks you'll have cascading blooms in fiery orange, red, and yellow that last all season. Both the flowers and the leaves are edible, carrying a pleasant peppery bite that transforms any summer salad into something special.

The Secret to Spectacular Bougainvillea Blooms Is Calculated Neglect
Bougainvillea's lavish color shows are triggered by stress — specifically, a period of reduced watering followed by a deep drink, which nudges the plant away from leafy growth and into glorious flowering mode. In a Mediterranean climate like Hermosa Beach or Portugal's Algarve, ease off watering in late spring, then water deeply once new growth appears and watch the papery blooms ignite in magenta, coral, or crimson. This is one plant relationship that will travel beautifully when you make your move across the Atlantic.

Science Confirms What Dog Owners Always Knew: Dogs Can Smell Illness
Multiple peer-reviewed studies have now confirmed that trained dogs can detect certain cancers — including melanoma, lung, and prostate cancer — through scent alone, with accuracy rates reaching 97% in controlled trials. Researchers believe dogs are picking up on volatile organic compounds released by tumor cells, a discovery that has inspired entirely new approaches to non-invasive early screening. In a week when a cancer vaccine made headlines, it is worth pausing to appreciate that humanity's oldest diagnostic partner still has a wet nose.
