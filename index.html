GOLDEN VISA
Portugal's Golden Visa remains open and actively processed through fund-based investments (€500K minimum in qualifying venture or real estate funds) and research or arts contributions. The direct urban real estate route closed in 2023, but the fund pathway continues to be a well-worn road to residency — and processing times have improved markedly in the past year.
✓
COST OF LIVING
Portugal continues to offer a quality of life that would cost 40–50% more in Southern California — good wine, excellent seafood, mild weather, and walkable neighborhoods included. Even as Lisbon has grown more expensive, it remains far more accessible than Hermosa Beach, and cities like Setúbal, Aveiro, and Braga offer extraordinary value for those willing to look beyond the capital.
~
NHR TAX REGIME
Portugal's original Non-Habitual Resident regime closed to new applicants in January 2024 and was replaced by IFICI — a narrower benefit aimed at researchers, tech professionals, and highly qualified workers. It still offers meaningful tax advantages on qualifying foreign income, but the eligible categories are fewer; consulting a Portuguese tax adviser well before you move is now essential rather than optional.
~
HOUSING MARKET
Rental prices in central Lisbon and Porto have risen sharply, with a one-bedroom in Lisbon's desirable neighborhoods now averaging €1,400–1,800/month — still well below comparable California rents, but rising. Secondary cities and inland towns offer dramatically better value, and for those planning a long stay, the buy-versus-rent calculus is increasingly tilting toward ownership.

Papua New Guinea Cuts Malaria Deaths by 92% Using Only Tools It Already Had
Without a new vaccine or any technological breakthrough, Papua New Guinea has reduced its malaria death rate from 13 per 100,000 inhabitants to barely 1 — using only bed nets, rapid testing, and consistent treatment protocols applied with real discipline. It is a powerful demonstration that what we already know, when actually implemented, can save lives at extraordinary scale. Sometimes the revolution is just doing the basics, brilliantly well.

In a quietly astonishing story from the plant kingdom, a group of plants called metallophytes — including wild pansies — are colonizing soil contaminated by centuries of lead and zinc mining across Europe and converting those toxins into clean organic compounds.
