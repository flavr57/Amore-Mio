Inside the Fund That Parks Your Cash and Pays You Daily
A money market fund is a type of mutual fund that holds only ultra-short-term, highly liquid instruments — U.S. Treasury bills, government agency debt, and overnight repurchase agreements — so your cash essentially never sits idle. Historically, yields on funds like Vanguard's VMFXX and Fidelity's SPAXX have ranged from near-zero during the long low-rate era of the 2010s to somewhere in the 4–5% vicinity when rates climbed significantly in recent years. The mechanism is elegantly simple: the fund pools deposited cash, buys these short-term instruments, collects the daily interest they generate, and credits it back to shareholders — often updating your balance every single day. The honest trade-off is that yields float with the federal funds rate, so what pays generously today can compress quickly after a rate cut, unlike a CD where you can lock a rate in. A concrete next step worth taking: pull the fund fact sheets for VMFXX and SPAXX directly from Vanguard and Fidelity's websites and compare their expense ratios, 7-day yield disclosures, and the exact mix of Treasury bills versus agency obligations held inside each. What Bougainvillea Really Needs to Deliver a Summer Full of Color
Bougainvillea's spectacular color comes not from flowers but from papery bracts — the true blooms are tiny white tubes tucked inside — and the key to maximum color is a little deliberate stress: let the soil dry out almost completely between waterings, since consistently wet roots suppress bloom production and invite rot. A south- or west-facing wall with generous reflected heat is your plant's happiest address, especially in a coastal or Mediterranean climate. In late summer, a light trim after the main flush of color encourages a second wave before autumn sets in.

The Peace Lily: A Low-Light Plant That Cleans Air and Aids Sleep
The peace lily (Spathiphyllum) earns its place on NASA's clean-air plant list by quietly absorbing benzene, formaldehyde, and trichloroethylene from indoor air — and it releases enough moisture to ease the dry-air sleep disruption that plagues air-conditioned rooms. It thrives in low light and forgives an occasional missed watering, though its glossy leaves will droop dramatically the moment it gets thirsty, which is unusually honest communication for a houseplant. A shaded corner of the bedroom or a northeast-facing windowsill is exactly where it wants to be.

Library Reading Dogs: The Four-Legged Tutors Who Never Judge a Stumble
Across library systems in the US and Europe, trained therapy dogs sit patiently while children read aloud to them — offering an audience that never winces at a stumbled word or a slow sentence.
