Standard Mileage, Actual Cost — and the Logbook That Decides It
When you're self-employed and driving for business, the IRS gives you two distinct methods for deducting vehicle costs on Schedule C: the standard mileage rate — which the agency resets each year and has generally run in the 55–70-cent-per-mile range over recent years — or the actual cost method, where you track fuel, insurance, repairs, and depreciation and deduct them in proportion to your business use. The actual cost path can shelter meaningfully more if you're running a newer or higher-value vehicle hard for work, since depreciation alone — potentially accelerated under Section 179 — can be substantial. The mechanism that gives both methods their power is double leverage: every deductible vehicle dollar lowers your net self-employment income, which trims both ordinary income tax and the self-employment tax in one move, so each deduction does more than it looks. The key trap to watch: choosing actual cost in the first year of a vehicle generally locks you out of the standard mileage option for that car going forward — and whichever method you pick, the IRS expects a contemporaneous mileage log, meaning date, destination, business purpose, and miles recorded as you drive, not reconstructed from memory come April. To go deeper, pull IRS Publication 463, 'Travel, Gift, and Car Expenses,' and read the instructions for Schedule C, Line 9 — those two sources define exactly what qualifies and what documentation would hold up under real scrutiny. Worth saying straight, the way a friend would: these rules shift with legislation and IRS guidance, and a CPA should look at your specific numbers before you change how you're filing. Pakistan has merged existing wildlife sanctuaries along the Indus River into a single continuous protected corridor stretching 250 miles — home to an estimated 440 rare river dolphins, extraordinary freshwater cetaceans found almost nowhere else on Earth. Bougainvillea in Late Summer: Let the Heat Work and Hold Back the Water
Bougainvillea is at its most spectacular in heat like this week's, and the counterintuitive secret is to resist watering — mild drought stress is precisely what cues the plant to push color rather than leaf. Wait until the soil is genuinely dry a few inches down, then water deeply and let it drain completely; in containers, this rhythm is especially important to prevent root rot during warm, humid nights. If you are already thinking about which plants make the move to Portugal, bougainvillea is a natural — it will feel entirely at home in Lisbon's light and reward you with months of uninterrupted bloom.

The Two Bedroom Plants Worth Every Square Foot for Sleep and Air
A well-chosen plant or two can measurably shift a bedroom's air quality — and sleep science has clarified which ones earn their place. Peace lilies (Spathiphyllum) filter benzene and formaldehyde while thriving in low light and forgiving stretches of neglect; snake plants (Sansevieria trifasciata) release oxygen at night rather than during the day, making them one of the rare specimens genuinely suited to a sleeping space rather than just tolerated in one. Both are compact, low-maintenance, and — useful to keep in mind — remarkably adaptable when it comes time to transplant them somewhere new.
