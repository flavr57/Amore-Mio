The Monthly Paycheck You Collect from Other People's Leases
Realty Income Corporation — traded under the ticker O, and officially self-styled 'The Monthly Dividend Company' — is a real estate investment trust that owns roughly 15,000 freestanding commercial properties leased to tenants like pharmacies, dollar stores, and convenience chains across the United States and Europe. Its dividend yield has historically run in the 4–6% range, loosely speaking, and crucially it pays monthly rather than quarterly, which gives it an income-stream feel that most stocks simply don't have. The engine underneath is the triple-net lease: tenants pay not just rent but also property taxes, insurance, and routine maintenance, stripping away the three biggest variable costs a landlord typically absorbs and making the cash flow unusually predictable. The honest trade-off is interest rate sensitivity — when Treasury yields climb, REIT prices tend to soften as income investors find safer alternatives, and Realty Income is not immune to that pull. A good next research thread: compare it directly with National Retail Properties (NNN) and Essential Properties Realty Trust (EPRT), then pull Realty Income's investor relations page and read through its historical occupancy rates and tenant concentration data — those two numbers tell you most of what you need to know about the durability of the income. Tomorrow we'll look at Prologis and the industrial warehouse REITs — a quieter corner of the market that's been riding the logistics buildout in some interesting ways.

September Is the Secret Month to Coax Your Orchid into a New Spike
As September nights cool — even gently, even in Southern California — phalaenopsis orchids quietly notice, and that mild temperature dip is often exactly what triggers them to set a new flower spike before winter. Move yours closer to a bright east- or south-facing window now, and let the potting medium dry out a little more between waterings, since roots that sit wet in cooler air are far more vulnerable to rot. Think of it less as intervention and more as paying attention: the plant is already looking for a reason to bloom, and your job is simply to make the conditions right.

What Researchers Keep Finding When They Study Therapy Dogs in Hospital Wards
Decades of animal-assisted therapy research keep arriving at the same surprising finding: the bonds that form between therapy dogs and patients become highly specific — a Lab that learns which rooms need a longer stay, a golden that settles quietly beside the ones who aren't speaking that day. Studies consistently show measurable drops in cortisol levels and self-reported pain during and after animal-assisted visits, and the dogs themselves display lower stress markers than companion animals in typical home settings.
