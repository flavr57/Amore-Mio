Getting Paid While You Wait: The Case for Cash-Secured Puts
A cash-secured put is a strategy where you sell someone the right to put shares of a stock to you at a specific price — the strike — while keeping enough cash in your account to actually buy those shares if it comes to that. On liquid, well-known stocks or broad ETFs like SPY or QQQ, the annualized premium income from selling puts has historically run somewhere in the 4–8% range, though that figure moves considerably with market volatility and how close to the money you're willing to sell. The mechanism is elegant: the option buyer is paying you for your willingness to own an asset at a price you've already decided makes sense — you're being compensated for a commitment you were going to make anyway. The honest catch is that you keep the premium regardless of outcome, but if the underlying drops sharply below your strike, the position can carry losses that far exceed what the premium collected. For a concrete research thread, look at how tastytrade or Schwab's options education center walks through strike selection and expiration windows on liquid index ETFs — their materials on put-selling mechanics are detailed and practical.
