How Your Business Structure Shapes Every Dollar You Keep
A sole proprietorship, an LLC, and an S-corp are three points on a spectrum — each one is a legal wrapper that changes how your income gets taxed, how exposed your personal assets are, and, for a self-employed parent with teenagers old enough to work, how you can legitimately put family members on payroll. As a sole prop, all net profit lands on your Form 1040 and attracts self-employment tax, which has historically run around 15.3% on the first tier of earnings — which is why so many self-employed people feel like they're paying taxes twice. An LLC doesn't automatically fix that: a single-member LLC is treated as a 'disregarded entity' by default, taxed identically to a sole prop, though it does build a real liability wall between your business and your personal assets. The structural shift happens when you file Form 2553 to elect S-corp status — now you pay yourself a documented 'reasonable salary,' run payroll on that amount, and any profits above that salary come out as distributions that skip self-employment tax entirely, with owners who've structured this correctly often reporting savings somewhere in the rough $5,000–$20,000 range, though the real number depends entirely on what the business earns. A good CPA will tell you plainly that these rules shift more than people expect and that you want someone to sign off on your specific setup before you file anything, because getting the 'reasonable salary' number wrong is exactly what the IRS looks for. The honest trade-off is compliance overhead: payroll processing, quarterly Form 941s, and proper bookkeeping can run $2,000–$4,000 a year, so the math only pencils out above a certain revenue level. Pull up IRS Publication 3402 ('Taxation of Limited Liability Companies') and the Form 2553 instructions, then search 'S-corp reasonable compensation IRS' to understand the line the agency actually draws. Bougainvillea in Late Summer: Coaxing One Final Magnificent Flush
If your bougainvillea has been quiet since its spring show, September is exactly the moment to nudge it back to life: a deliberate drought stress — cutting back water by roughly a third for two to three weeks — is often all it takes to trigger a fresh flush of bracts. Follow the dry spell with a low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus fertilizer (look for something in a 0-10-10 or 2-10-10 formula) and you'll typically see color returning within a month. In a climate like Hermosa Beach's — or the sun-soaked south of Portugal — bougainvillea asks for very little, just heat, good light, and the occasional gentle neglect.

Three Quietly Hardworking Houseplants That Earn Their Spot in the Bedroom
Snake plants (Sansevieria), peace lilies, and spider plants have all shown measurable air-filtering properties in controlled studies — absorbing compounds like formaldehyde and benzene that off-gas from furniture, paint, and synthetic textiles. The snake plant earns special appreciation in a bedroom because it releases oxygen at night rather than during the day, which is the reverse of most plants and makes it a genuinely useful bedside companion. All three are tolerant of mild neglect, modest light, and irregular watering — ideal for anyone whose attention is already on a transatlantic move.
