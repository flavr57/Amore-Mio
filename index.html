The Banks That Have Paid Dividends Through Wars, Recessions, and Pandemics
Canada's Big Six banks — Royal Bank (RY), TD Bank (TD), Scotiabank (BNS), Bank of Montreal (BMO), CIBC (CM), and National Bank (NA) — have paid uninterrupted dividends stretching back many decades, with some payout records topping a century without a single cut. Historically, their yields have generally run in a 4–6% range — a loose band that shifts with share prices and board decisions, so treat it as atmosphere rather than a target. The engine behind the consistency is Canada's concentrated banking oligopoly: six institutions share most of the domestic market with limited foreign competition, producing steady, predictable earnings that management has historically returned to shareholders rather than retained. For U.S.-based investors, the main thing to watch is withholding tax — Canadian dividends are typically taxed at source, and the rate varies by account type and treaty treatment, meaning where you hold these shares matters as much as which you pick. A concrete next step: line up the six tickers on TMX.com or a screener like Morningstar, compare their dividend history tables side by side, then separately look up the iShares Canadian Financial Monthly Income ETF (FIE.TO) as a bundled alternative worth understanding. Bougainvillea Loves This Heat — How to Coax a Second Flush of Blooms
With temperatures in the low 90s, bougainvillea is exactly in its element — this is the heat it was shaped by, and a well-established plant will often reward a hot September with a second wave of vivid bracts if you give it one deep, thorough watering and then let the soil dry almost completely before the next. Resist the urge to fertilize heavily now; a light application of low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus feed nudges the plant toward flowers rather than leafy growth. In Portugal you will find bougainvillea cascading freely against whitewashed walls well into October, so consider this warm week your practice round.
