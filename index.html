GOLDEN VISA
Portugal's Golden Visa program continues to attract strong interest through its qualifying investment fund route, with thresholds starting at €500,000 for eligible funds focused on low-density or inland areas. Processing times have been improving through 2026, and the D8 Digital Nomad Visa remains a popular parallel pathway for remote workers with proof of stable foreign income.

COST OF LIVING
Lisbon and Porto continue to see above-average inflation in groceries and dining, though both cities remain meaningfully more affordable than comparable Western European capitals. Coastal Algarve towns popular with expats have seen rental and dining costs rise 8–12% year-on-year, so budgeting conservatively — especially for housing — remains wise.

NHR TAX REGIME
Portugal's IFICI regime — the successor to the original Non-Habitual Resident program — offers qualifying new residents a flat 20% tax rate on Portuguese-source income for up to ten years. Retirees and remote workers relocating from non-EU countries should verify eligibility with a Lisbon-based fiscal advisor, as qualifying categories have been refined since the transition.
~
HOUSING MARKET
Property prices in Lisbon's historic neighborhoods have shown signs of plateauing, offering a window of relative stability after years of steep appreciation. Rental inventory across Porto and the Algarve remains tight heading into summer, so securing a long-term lease before arrival — ideally through a vetted local agent — is still strongly advisable.

Australia Bets on Tax Reform to Crack Open Its Unaffordable Housing Market
Australia has some of the world's most expensive homes relative to income, and the government has now moved to scrap long-standing tax breaks in hopes of making homeownership accessible to younger generations. Critics argue that reducing incentives for investment property owners could dampen new construction and worsen supply — a classic tension in housing reform that no country has fully resolved. It is a bold social wager, and economists around the world are watching to see whether demand or supply proves the more powerful lever.

WHO Monitors Hantavirus Closely, Finds No Signs of Wider Outbreak
The World Health Organization reports no evidence that a recent hantavirus cluster has grown into a broader outbreak, though officials note the situation could still evolve and additional confirmed cases may emerge. Hantavirus spreads primarily through contact with infected rodent droppings and rarely transmits between humans, which limits its pandemic potential significantly. The WHO's measured, transparent public communication here is itself a quiet story of global health infrastructure functioning as it should.

Independent Budget Office Finds Golden Dome Would Cost $1.2 Trillion
A nonpartisan budget office has pegged the price of the proposed 'Golden Dome' missile defense system at $1.2 trillion — nearly seven times the White House's initial estimate, and a figure that would dwarf almost any infrastructure program in American history. Beyond the staggering cost, analysts note the system would still be unlikely to defend against an all-out missile assault, raising pointed questions about what deterrence actually requires. It is a striking reminder that the hardest problems in security are rarely solved by the largest price tags.

A coordinated series of conservation projects in South Africa has cleared invasive trees and fish from more than 13,000 acres of vital native ecosystems, giving indigenous species room to breathe and thrive again. An Offshore Wind Turbine Now Powers a Sea-Cooled AI Data Center
A pioneering offshore wind firm has built a prototype turbine that houses a 12-megawatt data center inside its ballast tanks, using the surrounding seawater to cool the servers naturally and at no additional energy cost. The innovation elegantly solves two problems at once: channeling clean wind energy directly into AI computing while dispersing heat harmlessly into the ocean rather than warming the atmosphere on land. If the concept scales, it may be one of the more quietly brilliant infrastructure ideas to emerge from this decade.

Gardeners' World
Bougainvillea Blooms Brightest When You Learn to Step Away
Counterintuitively, bougainvillea produces its most spectacular color when mildly stressed: withhold water for two to three weeks before you want peak bloom, and the plant responds by throwing out brilliant bracts in beautiful self-preservation. In Portugal's warm, dry summers this maps perfectly onto the natural rainfall cycle — which is precisely why bougainvillea looks so outrageously alive cascading over whitewashed walls in the Algarve. Feed with a low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus fertilizer in early spring, then largely trust the plant to do the rest.
