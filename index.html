Your Home as a Venue: The 14-Day Rule That Pays You
The Augusta Rule — formally codified in IRS Section 280A(g) — allows you to rent your personal residence to your own business for up to 14 days each year, and the rental income you collect is entirely excluded from your personal taxable income. The scale of the benefit depends on local venue rates, but charging your business a fair market rate for hosting a strategy day, planning retreat, or board meeting at home can shelter anywhere from a few hundred to several thousand dollars, depending on what comparable meeting spaces command in your market. The mechanism is a genuine two-way move: your business deducts the rental payment as an ordinary business expense, while the homeowner side of the ledger owes no income tax on what it receives — the exclusion is explicit in the statute. The discipline required is documentation: the rate must reflect what a comparable venue would actually charge nearby, the business purpose must be legitimate and recorded, and you must stay strictly under 14 days, because crossing that threshold collapses the exemption entirely; the IRS has scrutinized aggressive applications in Tax Court, and a CPA who knows your situation should absolutely review this before you act on it, because the rules carry nuances that shift and that are fact-specific. To dig in, search the phrase 'IRC Section 280A(g) home rental business' or pull up IRS Publication 527, which covers residential rental property and specifically addresses the two-week exception. The World Map Gets a Fairer, Truer Picture of Africa
A United Nations resolution has moved to adopt map projections that more accurately reflect Africa's true geographic scale — correcting centuries of distortion from the Mercator projection, which systematically shrank the continent relative to Europe and North America. When the map changes, the mental model of who and what matters in the world begins to change with it.

New field surveys of the Chesapeake Bay show a meaningful rebound in blue crab populations after years of worrying decline that threatened both the estuary's ecology and the fishing communities built around the harvest. It is a quiet proof of concept: when humans pay patient attention and make measured interventions, coastal ecosystems have a real capacity to recover.
