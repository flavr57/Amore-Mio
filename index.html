Your Teenager's Summer Wages Could Quietly Seed a Retirement
A custodial Roth IRA is a retirement account opened in a minor's name and managed by a parent or guardian until the child reaches adulthood — and it can be funded only up to the amount the child actually earned that year, not a dollar more. The contribution ceiling has historically run in the low-to-mid thousands of dollars annually (the IRS adjusts the figure periodically, so check current guidance), but even a modest sum invested at 16 or 18 carries something adult investors simply cannot buy back: four or five extra decades of completely tax-free compounding. The mechanism is the Roth structure itself — contributions go in after tax, meaning every dollar of qualified growth eventually comes out in retirement with no tax bill, a feature that is most powerful precisely when someone is in a low bracket, which describes most teenagers rather well. The rule to watch is the earned income requirement: the child must have documented, legitimate income — a W-2 from an outside employer, or verifiable pay for real work in a family business — and contributions cannot exceed what they actually earned. This is territory where the rules do evolve and the family-business nuance is genuinely tricky, so loop in a CPA before acting on any of it. To research further, pull up IRS Publication 590-A and search 'custodial Roth IRA earned income minor' — those two threads answer most of the structural questions.
