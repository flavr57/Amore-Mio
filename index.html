The CD Upgrade: How a Brokerage Account Unlocks Rates Your Bank Won't Post
A brokered CD is a certificate of deposit purchased through a brokerage account — at Fidelity, Schwab, or Vanguard, for instance — rather than opened at a single bank's branch window. Because brokerages aggregate new-issue offerings from dozens of banks competing openly for your deposit, the rates available have historically run higher than what a local institution tends to advertise; in elevated-rate environments, brokered CDs have generally fallen somewhere in the 4–6% annualized range, though that figure moves considerably with the broader rate cycle. The mechanism is straightforward: banks need deposits to fund loans, and when they have to compete on a screen instead of relying on branch loyalty, they tend to pay more for the privilege. The honest catch is liquidity — unlike a bank CD where early withdrawal costs a penalty and you get your principal back, selling a brokered CD before maturity means finding a buyer on the secondary market, and if rates have risen since you bought, you'll likely sell at a discount to face value. A good next step is pulling up the new-issue CD screener at Fidelity or Schwab, sorting by term and yield, and comparing what FDIC-insured banks across the country are offering side by side — the spread between shopping the screen and taking whatever your branch posts can be eye-opening. Late-Summer Bougainvillea: A Little Stress Is the Whole Secret
Late August is when bougainvillea can start to look spent, but a little strategic tough love now pays off through fall — the key is easing back on water, because mild drought stress is precisely what signals the plant to push new bracts rather than leafy green growth. Follow that with a light trim of any long, non-flowering shoots to redirect energy into the flowering wood, and switch to a low-nitrogen, phosphorus-forward fertilizer (any 'bloom booster' formula works) applied every three to four weeks. If yours is in a container, make sure the pot is genuinely baking in full sun — bougainvillea saves its best color for the warmest roots.
