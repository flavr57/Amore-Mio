Cushion and Cap: What Buffer ETFs Actually Trade Away
Buffer ETFs — often branded as 'defined-outcome' or 'structured-outcome' ETFs — are exchange-traded funds built around an options overlay on a broad index, typically the S&P 500, that defines in advance exactly how much downside you are shielded from and how much upside you can capture over a specific outcome period, usually one year. The buffer typically covers the first 9 to 15 percent of losses, while deeper-protection series can shelter 20 to 30 percent; the upside cap has historically ranged from the mid-single digits to somewhere in the high teens, depending on where options were priced when the period last reset. The mechanism is an elegant exchange: the fund buys put options to build the floor and sells call options to finance that purchase — the cap is simply the cost of the cushion, expressed in foregone upside. The honest trade-off is that in a roaring bull year your gains stop at the cap while an unhedged index keeps climbing, and if markets fall harder than your buffer, you still absorb every point of loss beyond it. A concrete next step is to pull the live outcome-period fact sheets from Innovator ETFs or First Trust's Buffer ETF series, both of which publish current cap and buffer levels on any given day so you can see precisely what protection is costing in real-time market conditions.
