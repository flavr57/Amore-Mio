GOLDEN VISA
Portugal's Golden Visa program remains open to non-EU applicants through qualifying fund investments and other approved routes, even after direct real estate options were removed in 2023. The D8 Digital Nomad Visa continues to be a strong parallel pathway for location-independent professionals able to demonstrate stable remote income above the threshold.

COST OF LIVING
Lisbon and the Algarve remain noticeably pricier than five years ago, though everyday costs still run roughly 25–35% below comparable US coastal cities. Porto and interior towns continue to offer genuine value — savvy movers are increasingly looking beyond the obvious coastal hotspots to find the Portugal that existed before the boom.

NHR TAX REGIME
Portugal's IFICI regime — the successor to the original NHR — offers a flat 20% tax rate on Portuguese-source income for qualifying new residents, with certain foreign-source income potentially exempt for up to ten years. Timing your residency establishment carefully and working with a licensed Portuguese tax advisor remains essential to maximizing the benefit.

HOUSING MARKET
Rental demand in Lisbon continues to outpace supply, keeping one-bedroom prices in desirable neighborhoods firmly in the €1,200–€2,000/month range. Government rent-stabilization incentives are gradually encouraging more landlords back into the long-term market, though meaningful softening in the most sought-after areas may still be a year or two away.

James Webb Telescope Reveals a Planet Where Clouds Are Made of Rock
Astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope have unveiled the breathtaking atmospheric chemistry of WASP 94A b, a gas giant where clouds are literally composed of vaporized rock — silicates that condense into stone each night and melt back into cloud every morning as the planet rotates. The discovery pushes our understanding of exotic planetary atmospheres into extraordinary new territory. As one researcher noted with perfect California wit, it's like the June gloom over Southern California — just with geology instead of marine layer.

Nasturtiums: The Cheerful Edible Flower That Practically Grows Itself
Nasturtiums are one of gardening's most generous gifts — sow them in poor soil, give them sun, and they'll cascade into fiery oranges and golds from late spring through autumn with almost no effort on your part. Both the flowers and the leaves are edible, carrying a delightful peppery bite that elevates any salad, and they double as exceptional companion plants that draw aphids away from more delicate neighbors. In Portugal, they naturalize beautifully in terracotta pots on a sun-drenched terrace, thriving — as the best things do — on benign neglect.
