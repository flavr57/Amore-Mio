The Warehouses Powering the World Are Worth a Closer Look
Industrial REITs like Prologis (ticker: PLD) own and operate the sprawling network of distribution centers, fulfillment warehouses, and last-mile logistics hubs that keep the global supply chain moving — from Amazon megaplexes outside Atlanta to cold-chain facilities ringing major ports. As an income vehicle, industrial REITs have historically offered dividend yields in a loose 2–4% range, though Prologis has often run toward the lower end of that spectrum as its share price appreciated sharply through the e-commerce boom years; treat that range as rough orientation, not a forecast. The mechanism is elegantly simple: companies pay rent for warehouse space, that rent gets pooled, and a REIT's mandated 90% payout flows through to shareholders as dividends. The honest trade-off is that this sector ran very hot through the early 2020s, and a meaningful softening in e-commerce demand or a rise in vacancy rates across key logistics corridors could compress both rents and valuations at the same time. A concrete next step is to pull Prologis's most recent investor supplement at their IR page, then compare it with Rexford Industrial (REXR), which focuses specifically on infill Southern California markets, and look up industrial-focused REIT ETFs — the Pacer Benchmark Industrial Real Estate SCTR ETF (INDS) is one worth putting on your research list. Tomorrow we'll get into something that hits closer to home: hiring your own teenagers into the business and putting them on a real payroll — legally and to surprisingly meaningful tax advantage.

Orchids Know Autumn Is Coming — Here's How to Help Them Through It
As days shorten and indoor light levels begin to dip, most orchids — especially the ubiquitous phalaenopsis — naturally want to slow down, and overwatering during this transition is by far the most common way to lose an otherwise healthy plant. Let the potting medium dry almost completely between waterings, and check the roots through the clear nursery pot: silver-gray roots are thirsty, plump green roots are satisfied. Moving your orchid closer to a bright south- or east-facing window right now gives it the light cue it needs to build energy for next spring's flower spike.

Bougainvillea Blooms Best When You Treat It a Little Tough
Bougainvillea rewards restraint — kept slightly drought-stressed between waterings and somewhat root-bound in a snugger pot than you'd instinctively choose, it channels energy away from lush foliage and into those spectacular papery bracts. If yours has been all green and no color, try pulling back on water and fertilizer for a few weeks while giving it as much direct sun as your space allows. A mild stress period heading into autumn can prime it beautifully for a vivid burst of color when warmth returns in spring.

It Turns Out the Morning Dog Walk Is Quietly Remaking Your Health
A growing body of longitudinal research consistently finds that dog owners clock significantly more daily steps, report lower rates of loneliness, and show measurably better cardiovascular markers than non-owners — almost entirely because of the non-negotiable morning walk that a dog imposes on your schedule with cheerful, tail-wagging authority. Beyond the fitness metrics, that walk turns out to be social infrastructure in disguise: dog owners are far more likely to exchange words with neighbors and strangers, building the kind of loose neighborhood fabric that correlates strongly with long-term wellbeing. The dog, it seems, is not just a companion but something closer to a prescription.
