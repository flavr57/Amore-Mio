Corporate Bonds That Pay You: Getting to Know LQD and VCIT
LQD (the iShares iBoxx $ Investment Grade Corporate Bond ETF, from BlackRock) and VCIT (Vanguard's Intermediate-Term Corporate Bond ETF) are two of the most widely held exchange-traded funds built around investment-grade corporate debt — bonds issued by financially solid companies like Apple, JPMorgan, and Comcast. Funds in this category have historically run distribution yields somewhere in the 3–6% range, though the actual figure shifts meaningfully depending on where interest rates happen to sit at any given moment. The mechanism is elegantly simple: corporations pay regular coupon interest on their bonds, the ETF collects those payments across hundreds of holdings, and distributes them to shareholders as monthly income. The honest trade-off is duration risk — when rates rise, the net asset value of the fund tends to fall, which matters if you need to sell before rates stabilize, though patient income-seekers often ride that out. A concrete research thread to pull: bring up the official fact sheets on iShares.com and Vanguard.com side by side and compare effective duration, average credit quality, yield-to-maturity, and expense ratios — those four numbers tell most of the story. October Is the Ideal Moment to Repot and Refresh Your Orchid
As autumn light softens and temperatures settle, orchids benefit from a close look at their growing medium — bark or sphagnum that has broken down over a year or two loses drainage and can suffocate the aerial roots that need air circulation to thrive. Gently unpot, trim any dead roots back to healthy tissue, and repot in fresh orchid bark in a clear plastic pot so you can monitor root color from the outside: silvery-green means it's time to water, bright green means moisture is still present. A clean repot now sets the plant up for strong spike development through the cooler months ahead.

Chaser, a Border Collie studied by psychologist John Pilley at Wofford College in South Carolina, learned the proper names of more than 1,000 individual objects — more than any other non-human animal on record — and could sort them by category, shape, and function using reasoning rather than rote memory. Her story, built entirely through years of gentle, play-based interaction, quietly upended long-held assumptions about the ceiling of animal cognition.
