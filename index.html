How an Accountable Plan Turns Personal Expenses Into Business Deductions
An accountable plan is an IRS-recognized arrangement that lets you formally reimburse yourself for the business-use portion of personal assets — the car making client runs, the home office, the phone that never actually stops being a work device. The shelter it creates is genuinely useful: documented business use of a personal vehicle alone can shift hundreds or even thousands of dollars a year out of taxable income and into a deductible business expense, though the real number depends entirely on your actual usage and records. The mechanism is elegant: you document the business-use percentage, write yourself a check from the business account, and that money comes back to you tax-free while the business takes the deduction. The word 'accountable' is doing real work in that name — the IRS requires contemporaneous mileage logs and receipts, and a plan without them gets recharacterized as taxable compensation faster than you would like. That said, have a CPA look at this before you put it in motion, because these rules do shift and the line between a clean reimbursement and a taxable draw is thinner than the publications make it look. To dig in, search 'accountable plan reimbursement' and pull up IRS Publication 463, which covers vehicle, travel, and home office rules in one readable document — and if your 16- or 18-year-old is legitimately helping in the business, note that the same framework can extend to them as well.
