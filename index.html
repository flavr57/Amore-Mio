Two Funds That Put the Whole REIT Market in One Trade
VNQ (Vanguard Real Estate ETF) and SCHH (Schwab U.S. REIT ETF) are broad index funds that track the U.S. real estate investment trust market — owning slices of hundreds of companies that hold everything from apartment complexes and data centers to warehouses and cell towers. Both have historically distributed income in roughly the 3–5% range, though yields shift with interest rates and property valuations, so treat any figure as a loose historical reference rather than a guarantee. The underlying mechanism is structural: REITs are legally required to distribute at least 90% of their taxable income to shareholders, which is what pushes income yields higher than a typical equity index. The honest trade-off right now is interest rate sensitivity — when the 10-year Treasury climbs, REIT prices tend to feel the pressure as income-seeking capital has a competing alternative. A useful next step is pulling each fund's fact sheet directly on Vanguard.com and Schwab.com to compare expense ratios, sector weightings, and how their top ten holdings actually differ.
