How Active Management Hunts for Your Monthly Dividend Check
DIVO — the Amplify CWP Enhanced Dividend Income ETF — is an actively managed fund that holds a concentrated portfolio of roughly 20 to 25 blue-chip dividend-paying U.S. equities, then layers a selective covered call strategy on top to generate additional premium income paid out monthly. Over its history, the fund's total distribution yield (dividends plus call premium) has generally run somewhere in the 4–6% range, though that figure shifts with market conditions and is worth verifying fresh before drawing any conclusions. The covered call overlay is the central mechanism: the manager sells short-term call options on select holdings, collecting the premium as cash income in exchange for capping some potential upside on those positions during sharp rallies. The honest trade-off is that active management costs more than a passive index fund, and the covered call approach means DIVO tends to lag in strong bull markets while cushioning somewhat in sideways or choppy ones. For a useful next step, pull DIVO's fund fact sheet from Amplify's site alongside JEPI — JPMorgan's Equity Premium Income ETF — and compare how each constructs its overlay: DIVO is selective and quality-focused, JEPI more systematic and broad, which produces meaningfully different yield and volatility profiles worth understanding side by side. Tomorrow we turn to VYMI, Vanguard's international high-dividend ETF — and why the withholding tax wrinkle hiding inside it matters more than most people realize before they reach for the yield.

Baby Macs Is Thriving Thanks to a Mechanical Heart Called the Berlin Heart

September Is the Gentle Moment to Repot Your Phalaenopsis Orchid
As the sharpest heat of summer softens along the Southern California coast, late September opens a forgiving window to repot phalaenopsis orchids before they push their winter growth flush — look for gray, wrinkled roots or bark that has broken down to near-compost as your signal that it is time. Fresh medium, fine or medium bark, gives the roots room to breathe and the plant a nutritional reset heading into the cooler months. After repotting, hold off on watering for about a week to let any trimmed root tips callus before resuming your usual rhythm.

Stress Your Bougainvillea a Little and Watch It Burst Into Color
Bougainvillea blooms most brilliantly when it is slightly stressed — too much water and a heavy nitrogen feed produces exuberant green growth at the direct expense of those vivid papery bracts. As September settles in, ease off the fertilizer and let the soil dry more thoroughly between waterings to coax one final wave of color before the days shorten noticeably. A light tidy of any leggy stems now will also set the plant up for a clean, shapely flush come spring.

In wards across the country, trained therapy dogs visit patients during chemotherapy, sit quietly with children before surgery, and offer the kind of unhurried, wordless comfort that medication simply cannot provide — and the research behind the practice grows more robust each year.
