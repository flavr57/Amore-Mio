Self-Storage Is Unglamorous. That's Exactly Why It Pays.
Public Storage (NYSE: PSA) is the largest self-storage real estate investment trust in the United States, owning and operating thousands of facilities across the country and collecting rent from millions of people who have decided their belongings need a home outside their home. As a REIT, it is legally required to distribute at least 90% of its taxable income to shareholders, and its dividend yield has historically run somewhere in the 3–5% range — treat that loosely, as current yield moves with the share price. The engine behind that income stream is behavioral: people rent a unit, fill it, and then quietly keep paying month after month because clearing it out feels worse than the monthly charge, creating unusually sticky, low-churn recurring revenue with modest capital requirements per square foot. The honest trade-off is that self-storage demand is sensitive to housing market velocity — when people stop moving, they stop needing storage — and rising interest rates compress REIT valuations across the board. A productive next step would be to pull the investor relations pages for PSA alongside its major peers — Extra Space Storage (EXR), CubeSmart (CUBE), and National Storage Affiliates (NSA) — and compare occupancy trends, payout histories, and debt levels across a full market cycle. Tomorrow: a tax strategy that some parent-entrepreneurs use to legitimately put their kids on the payroll — the IRS rules around it are more interesting than you'd expect.

October Is the Quiet Window to Reset and Repot Your Orchids
As summer heat softens into fall, phalaenopsis orchids enter one of their best windows for repotting — roots that have been quietly circling their containers all season will respond beautifully to fresh bark medium and a slightly roomier pot. The timing matters: the work you do now sets up the next bloom cycle, which for most phals arrives in winter when they need you least. While you're at it, check the air roots: healthy ones are silvery-green at the tip, and if yours have gone brown and papery, a pebble tray filled with water will bring the humidity back up without soaking the roots.
