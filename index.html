GOLDEN VISA
Portugal's Golden Visa remains open for qualifying investment fund and cultural contributions, offering a clear path to residency and eventual EU citizenship. The D8 Digital Nomad Visa continues to attract location-independent workers, requiring proof of monthly income of roughly €3,480 to qualify.

COST OF LIVING
Lisbon and Porto have seen steady price increases over the past two years, with groceries and dining roughly 20–30% higher than in 2022 — though still well below equivalent costs in most of Western Europe or coastal California. The Algarve and inland regions remain noticeably more affordable for those with flexibility on location.
✓
NHR TAX REGIME
Portugal's original Non-Habitual Resident program closed to new applicants on January 1, 2024, but its successor — the IFICI regime — offers a flat 20% tax rate on Portuguese-source income for qualifying professionals, researchers, and investors for a 10-year period. Those who registered under the old NHR before the deadline retain their full benefits.
~
HOUSING MARKET
Rental prices in Lisbon have stabilized somewhat following government intervention, but demand continues to outpace supply in central neighborhoods. Buyers and renters willing to explore secondary cities like Setúbal, Braga, or Évora are finding considerably better value — a trend worth watching as more expats discover the same secret.

WHO Declares Ebola Emergency, and Global Health Systems Move Swiftly
The World Health Organization has declared the ongoing Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo a Public Health Emergency of International Concern, mobilizing international resources and coordination even as officials note the outbreak — with roughly 246 cases and 80 deaths — does not rise to the level of a pandemic. The declaration is less a signal of alarm than a mechanism: it unlocks funding, accelerates vaccine deployment, and triggers the kind of coordinated global response that in past outbreaks has proven decisive. The speed of the institutional reaction is itself a quiet measure of how much the world has learned since 2014.

Seawater DNA Is Now a Window Into the Health of Wild Dolphins
Scientists have demonstrated for the first time that environmental DNA — genetic material floating freely in seawater — can be used to assess the health, diversity, and population size of wild dolphin communities along the American coastline. The technique requires only a water sample rather than close contact with the animals, opening a new era of non-invasive marine conservation monitoring. It is the kind of quiet scientific breakthrough that makes the ocean feel a little less unknowable — and its inhabitants a little more protected.

Science Confirms: Museums and Music Concerts May Slow How You Age
A new study finds that people who regularly engage with arts and culture — attending concerts, visiting museums, reading, or simply listening to music — show measurably slower biological aging, roughly 4% less than their less culturally active peers. Researchers believe the cognitive engagement, emotional stimulation, and social connection that accompany cultural participation may work on the body much as physical exercise does. Consider this your Sunday morning permission slip to book that gallery visit you have been meaning to make.

If you have a fence or wall crying out for life, a well-chosen climbing plant can achieve in a single season what takes other plants years. Bougainvillea — that riot of magenta and papery bracts so beloved in Mediterranean and Portuguese gardens — ranks among the fastest and most dramatic choices for warm climates, capable of blanketing a large surface within two to three seasons.
