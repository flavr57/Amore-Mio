The Brands in Every Bathroom and Pantry That Pay You Back
Consumer staples companies are the quiet workhorses of the income investor's portfolio — think Colgate-Palmolive and PepsiCo, whose products move off shelves in boom times and recessions alike, generating the kind of cash flows that make consistent dividend payments possible. Historically, dividend yields in this sector have run somewhere in the 2–4% range, though individual companies vary and those figures shift with market prices, so treat any number as a rough starting point rather than a guarantee. The mechanism is elegantly straightforward: because toothpaste, snacks, and household staples are non-negotiable purchases, the revenues that fund those dividends stay remarkably stable compared to, say, a semiconductor company or an airline. The honest trade-off is that these are mature businesses with limited growth runways, and in a roaring bull market they will often lag the broader index considerably — you are trading upside for steadiness. A useful thread to pull is the Consumer Staples Select Sector SPDR Fund (XLP): compare its dividend history and volatility profile against the S&P 500 across different market cycles, then look at how individual names like Colgate (ticker: CL) and PepsiCo (PEP) have sustained — and grown — their payments across decades. Tomorrow we look at Verizon and AT&T, and why the highest yield in the room can sometimes be the one that should make you most cautious.

September Is Bougainvillea's Sweet Spot — Here's How to Keep It Blazing
Along the Southern California coast, September sits perfectly in bougainvillea's blooming window: warm enough to sustain vivid color, but the ideal moment to ease off heavy fertilizer and let the plant begin hardening before cooler nights arrive. One of the most reliable tricks is allowing the soil to dry slightly between waterings — mild stress actually triggers more intense bract production, which is why the most spectacular specimens you have ever seen were almost certainly growing somewhere warm and just a little dry. For anyone planning a future life in Portugal, where bougainvillea drapes entire whitewashed facades in magenta and coral, this is a plant well worth befriending now.
