The Tax Bracket That Lets Your Investment Gains Grow Tax-Free
The IRS taxes long-term capital gains — profits on assets held for more than a year — at three possible federal rates: 0%, 15%, or 20%, depending on your taxable income. The 0% threshold has historically landed somewhere in the range of roughly $40,000–$50,000 for single filers and around $80,000–$100,000 for joint filers, though those numbers adjust with inflation each year and should never be assumed stable. If your business income runs lighter in a given year — or you're simply planning ahead — you may find room inside that bracket to sell appreciated positions in a taxable brokerage account, realize those gains, and owe nothing federally: that deliberate move is called gain harvesting, and it resets your cost basis at no tax cost. The watch-out is that realized gains add to your adjusted gross income, which can affect financial aid calculations for your 18-year-old, ACA premium credits, and your overall bracket picture — and because Congress has adjusted these thresholds before and can again, a CPA should absolutely run the full numbers with you before you act, not after. To pull this thread yourself, search IRS Publication 550 ('Investment Income and Expenses') and the Schedule D instructions, and look specifically for the phrase '0 percent capital gain rate' alongside the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet in the Form 1040 instructions. The Small Mistakes That Prevent Seeds from Germinating in the First Place
Starting seeds successfully comes down to a handful of repeatable details: use fresh seed compost rather than potting mix, keep temperatures consistent rather than letting trays fluctuate between warm and cold, and resist overwatering — damping off, the fungal collapse that kills seedlings overnight, is almost always a moisture problem. Expert grower Sally Nex recommends covering freshly sown seeds with a thin layer of vermiculite rather than compost, which holds just enough moisture without smothering. Once germination begins, the transition to bright light is as critical as the warmth — seedlings that reach for a distant window become weak and leggy before they ever get started.

What Your Orchid's Leaves Are Telling You — and How to Respond
Yellowing leaves on a Phalaenopsis orchid are rarely an emergency — most often they signal too much direct sun or inconsistent watering, both straightforward to correct. Move your orchid to bright but indirect light (an east-facing window is a classic sweet spot), and water only when the bark or moss medium has dried out completely, roughly once a week in winter. If the yellow is creeping in from the leaf edges rather than the center, your orchid is likely rootbound and ready for a fresh pot — spring, when new roots are actively pushing, is the ideal moment to repot.

The Quiet Gift of Puppy Raisers: Two Years of Love, Then Letting Go
Across the country, volunteer families raise guide dog puppies for roughly two years — teaching them manners, socialization, and trust — then surrender them so they can transform a stranger's life forever. Organizations like The Seeing Eye, America's oldest guide dog school, have been matching people with trained dogs since 1929, and their puppy-raising networks are as essential as the trainers themselves. It's one of the most quietly selfless things a family can do: loving something completely, then letting it go because someone else needs it more.
