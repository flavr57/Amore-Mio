How Qualified Dividends Get Taxed at a Fraction of Your Business Rate
Qualified dividends are stock dividends that clear an IRS holding-period test and get taxed at long-term capital gains rates rather than ordinary income rates — a meaningful split between two categories of investment income that can look identical on a brokerage statement. For a self-employed business owner already paying both income tax and the full self-employment tax on every dollar of profit, the gap matters: historically, the spread between the top ordinary rate and the qualified dividend rate has run roughly 17–20 percentage points, and for anyone also starting to think about college funding, that same gap makes custodial UTMA accounts worth a conversation with your CPA. The mechanism is clean — hold a qualifying stock through its ex-dividend date (the IRS requires at least 61 days within the surrounding 121-day window) and that dividend converts from ordinary to preferential treatment, taxed the same as a long-term capital gain. The honest watch-out is that not everything qualifies: REIT distributions, money market interest, and dividends from certain foreign companies are ordinary income regardless of holding period, so the savings don't apply uniformly across a portfolio. As a friend who also happens to do taxes would tell you, these rules shift and brackets adjust with every Congress, so run the actual numbers with a CPA before restructuring anything around this idea. To pull the thread, search IRS Publication 550 — 'Investment Income and Expenses' — and find the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet inside the Form 1040 instructions; it shows exactly how the rate preference works in practice. What Your Orchid Is Telling You When It Drops November Blooms
Phalaenopsis orchids often shed blooms in autumn not from distress but from a natural resting response to shorter days and cooler nights — in Hermosa Beach, with temperatures dipping into the low 60s, that seasonal shift is enough to trigger exactly this. Rather than reaching for fertilizer, cut the spent spike just above the second node from the base, then move the plant to your brightest indirect light and let it rest until January. The reward for patience is typically a fresh flush of blooms in late winter, often more abundant than the last.

November Is the Right Moment to Shape Your Bougainvillea for Next Year
Bougainvillea blooms on new wood, so a light prune now — removing the longest trailing stems by about a third and cutting back any crossing or crowded growth — sets up a fuller, more vigorous flush of color come spring. In coastal Southern California, November's mild temperatures and reduced watering mean the plant is naturally slowing, making it the ideal window for shaping without stressing the root system. Leave a few mature stems intact; they're the backbone the new season's color will hang from.
