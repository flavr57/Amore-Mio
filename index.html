SCHD: The Quality Screen That Earns Your Dividends the Slow, Steady Way
SCHD is the Schwab US Dividend Equity ETF, a passive fund tracking the Dow Jones US Dividend 100 Index — a basket of one hundred US companies chosen not simply for paying dividends but for passing a multi-factor quality screen that weighs cash flow to debt, return on equity, dividend yield, and five-year dividend growth rate. Historically, the fund has run in roughly the 3–4% yield range, though that figure shifts with price movements and distribution changes over time. The mechanism that produces the income is the screen itself: by requiring five consecutive years of dividend payments plus those quality metrics, the index tilts toward financially durable businesses rather than the highest current yielders, which often signal a company under stress. The honest trade-off is that this quality filter excludes entire sectors — REITs are out completely — and in roaring bull markets SCHD has tended to lag the broader S&P 500 on total return; it tends to earn its keep more in flat or down environments. A useful next step is to pull the Dow Jones US Dividend 100 Index methodology document directly and then run a side-by-side comparison against iShares' DVY, which screens differently and skews toward higher immediate yields with less of a quality overlay — the contrast illuminates exactly what SCHD's filter is buying you. Tomorrow we're looking at VYM, Vanguard's High Dividend Yield ETF, which takes a different approach entirely — market-cap weighting a much wider pool of payers — and it's worth understanding alongside SCHD.

Late Summer Is Bougainvillea's Moment — Here Is How to Make the Color Last
Bougainvillea blooms most extravagantly when it is slightly stressed: a touch root-bound, watered infrequently, and soaking in direct sun with good drainage beneath it. If the papery bracts are fading earlier than you'd like, the usual culprit is too much water rather than too little — let the soil go completely dry between waterings and the plant will reward you.
