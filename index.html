The Paper Trail Behind Every Defensible Schedule C
Schedule C is the form sole proprietors and single-member LLCs use to report business income and deductions — and every line on it needs a receipt, a contemporaneous log, or a written record that could survive a skeptical stranger's review. The categories that carry real weight — home office, vehicle mileage, equipment, contractor payments, and wages paid to your teenagers if they do genuine work for the business — can shelter tens of thousands of dollars in taxable income a year, though the exact range depends entirely on the size and nature of the operation. The mechanism is clean: the IRS allows deductions for ordinary and necessary business expenses, but 'ordinary and necessary' is your burden to prove, not theirs to disprove. The watch-out is that contemporaneous records — logs made in real time, not reconstructed months later — carry far more weight than approximations if questions ever arise. A solid starting point is IRS Publication 334 (Tax Guide for Small Business) and Publication 463 (Travel, Gift, and Car Expenses); if your teens are on payroll, also pull IRS Publication 15 on employment taxes and search the phrase 'Schedule C substantiation requirements.' These rules shift with legislation and IRS guidance, so treat this as a map of what to bring to your CPA, not a filing plan to act on alone. The World Meteorological Organization has issued a stark warning that the next El Niño cycle may be unusually severe, with the potential to disrupt rainfall patterns, intensify droughts and floods, and strain food and water systems across multiple continents at once. Science Has Confirmed What Gardeners Knew: The Blackbird Wins
A large survey of bird enthusiasts and acoustic researchers has found that the common blackbird — the same bird singing from rooftops across Europe at dusk — produces the most beautiful songs in the avian world, and the science offers a satisfying explanation. Blackbirds are vocal learners who refine their songs across a lifetime, improvising and adding new phrases in ways that create genuine melodic variation rather than simple repetition. The next time one serenades you from a garden wall in Portugal, you have every reason to stop mid-sentence and just listen.

September Is When Your Orchid Is Quietly Planning Its Next Bloom
As coastal temperatures ease into September, phalaenopsis orchids get a biological cue they have been waiting for — a modest drop in nighttime temperature is one of the primary triggers for new spike development, so the cooling air is your ally right now. Keep them in bright, indirect light and let the potting medium approach dryness between waterings; this slight stress nudges the plant toward blooming over leafy growth. Once a month, flush the roots thoroughly with plain water to clear accumulated fertilizer salts, which can burn roots silently over time in bark mix.

To Get More Bougainvillea Blooms, Give It a Little Less of Everything
Bougainvillea blooms most extravagantly when it feels a touch of stress — dialing back water through September and switching to a low-nitrogen, high-phosphorus fertilizer tells the plant to spend its energy on flower bracts rather than leafy sprawl. In Hermosa Beach's mild climate, a confident prune after the current flush of color will set you up for a spectacular spring show; the plant responds to being cut back the way a good jazz musician responds to silence — with something better. Resist the urge to overwater when the bracts drop, because dormancy and death look surprisingly similar, and only one of them needs your intervention.
