Buying Treasury Bills Directly from the Source, at Auction
TreasuryDirect.gov is the U.S. Treasury's own retail portal, where any American can buy Treasury bills directly — no brokerage, no middleman, no commission. Over recent years, short-duration T-bill yields have generally run in the 4–5% range, though in prior low-rate environments they sat much closer to 0.5–2%, so the actual return depends heavily on when you're buying. The mechanism is an auction: the Treasury sells bills at a discount to face value, and the gap between your purchase price and the face value you collect at maturity is your return — treated as ordinary income and exempt from state and local taxes. The honest friction is the platform itself: TreasuryDirect is functional but decidedly unglamorous, reinvestment requires active attention, and early exit means selling on the secondary market rather than redeeming directly. A concrete next step is to visit TreasuryDirect.gov, pull up their auction calendar, and look at an upcoming 4-week or 13-week bill — seeing real discount rates on a live auction gives you an immediate feel for what the market is actually paying. It is a sharp reminder that the chokepoints of global commerce are ultimately governed by rain.

Bougainvillea in Coastal Fog: What Your Plant Actually Needs
Bougainvillea thrives in conditions very similar to both Hermosa Beach and the Algarve — full sun, well-drained soil, and occasional deep watering rather than frequent shallow drinks — but fog-heavy mornings can invite root rot if the soil stays wet between sessions. Let the top inch or two dry out completely before watering again, and if your plant looks lush but stubbornly refuses to bloom, try holding back on both water and fertilizer: mild stress is often what finally triggers that spectacular burst of color. In Portugal, bougainvillea grows almost feral along sun-drenched walls, so your California plant is excellent practice for what's coming.
