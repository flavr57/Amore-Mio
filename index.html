How the I-Bond Keeps Your Savings Running Alongside Inflation
Series I savings bonds are U.S. Treasury securities purchased directly through TreasuryDirect.gov, built around a hybrid interest rate: a fixed component that stays constant for the bond's life, layered on top of an inflation adjustment tied to the Consumer Price Index that resets every six months. Their composite rate has historically ranged from near zero during low-inflation stretches to well above 8% during periods of sharp price increases — context-dependent to a degree that few savings instruments match. The appeal is purely mechanical: when inflation rises, the bond's rate rises with it, protecting purchasing power in a way a conventional fixed-rate savings account simply cannot replicate. The honest catch is illiquidity — I-bonds cannot be redeemed at all during the first twelve months, and exiting before five years costs you three months of interest. A natural research thread is TreasuryDirect.gov's historical rate chart, which maps every composite rate since 1998 and breaks out the fixed and inflation components separately — a quick way to see exactly how the two pieces have moved across different economic climates. Tomorrow we'll look at certificates of deposit and the CD ladder strategy — a clever way to stay flexible while still chasing better yields.

Your Skull Is Running Secret Immune Operations to Defend Your Brain
Scientists studying mouse skull bone marrow have identified specialized 'immune hubs' — clusters of immune cells that appear to actively monitor and defend the brain rather than simply circulating passively through the bloodstream. Similar structures have been found in human skulls, opening a promising new line of inquiry into how the brain protects itself from infection, inflammation, and neurological disease. If the function of these human hubs is confirmed, the finding could reshape thinking around conditions from multiple sclerosis to Alzheimer's.

Nearly a decade after the mass displacement of the Rohingya people, renewed international talk of repatriation has yet to produce the conditions — accountability, physical safety, legal recognition — that would make any real return possible. The structures required for homecoming remain absent despite diplomatic statements suggesting progress, leaving close to a million people in a prolonged, stateless limbo. The story continues to be one of the most unresolved human rights crises of this century, and a quiet test of international resolve.

It is the kind of slow, patient, hands-on conservation that quietly changes the trajectory of a species.

Bougainvillea's Secret to Its Most Spectacular Color? A Calculated Drought
Bougainvillea's vivid displays are actually papery bracts rather than true flowers, and coaxing the most dazzling color requires something counterintuitive: a deliberate period of drought stress in late summer, withholding water for two to four weeks until the soil goes quite dry between waterings. This signals the plant to shift energy into reproduction, and the flush of brilliant color that follows is the reward for a little strategic neglect. The technique works just as well in Portugal as it does in Southern California — bougainvillea thrives on both coasts, and tough love is the shared language.
